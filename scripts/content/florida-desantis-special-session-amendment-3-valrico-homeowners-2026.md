On November 4, 2026, the day after Florida voters approved Amendment 3 by a 62 percent supermajority, Governor Ron DeSantis announced he would call lawmakers into a special legislative session before the end of his term on January 5, 2027. The session's primary purpose: implement Amendment 3's expanded homestead exemption by January 1 and limit local governments from raising other fees or taxes to offset the revenue loss. That announcement has direct financial implications for Valrico homeowners -- and it also signals the beginning of a significant political transition that will reshape Florida housing policy in 2027 and beyond.

## What the Special Session Actually Does

Amendment 3, which passed November 3 with 62 percent of the vote, increases the non-school homestead property tax exemption in two stages:

- **January 1, 2027:** Exemption rises from $50,000 to $150,000
- **January 1, 2028:** Exemption rises further to $250,000, with inflation adjustments beginning in 2029

The amendment applies to every taxing authority except school districts. That detail is critical. Your school millage -- the largest single component of your Hillsborough County property tax bill -- does not change under Amendment 3. The savings are real but confined to the non-school portion.

Here is why DeSantis had to call a special session: Amendment 3 as approved by voters is a constitutional change, but it does not automatically prevent local governments from responding by raising other millage rates, service fees, or assessments to maintain their revenue levels. Without legislative guardrails, some counties and municipalities could technically offset the entire homestead tax savings with compensating fee increases.

The special session DeSantis announced will specifically:

1. **Set the implementation schedule** for the $150,000 exemption taking effect January 1, 2027
2. **Restrict local government workarounds** -- limiting the ability of counties and municipalities to raise other taxes or fees to compensate for the reduced revenue from the expanded exemption
3. **Establish a bridge funding pool** to provide grants to local governments for core services, including rural law enforcement, to cushion the revenue impact

According to [CBS News Miami's reporting on the DeSantis announcement](https://www.cbsnews.com/miami/news/desantis-special-session-amendment-3-property-tax-implement-before-january/), DeSantis said the session must implement the amendment before he leaves office. Governor-elect Byron Donalds takes office January 5, 2027, meaning the outgoing DeSantis administration wants the implementation locked in before the transition.

## The Dollar Math: What Valrico Homeowners Actually Save

The [full Amendment 3 savings analysis for Valrico](/blog/florida-amendment-3-passed-november-2026-valrico-tax-savings/) covers the numbers in depth, but here is the essential summary for Hillsborough County property owners.

Hillsborough County's combined non-school millage rate runs approximately 11.0 to 11.5 mills for most unincorporated Valrico properties. At those rates:

**Valrico 33594 (Bloomingdale, Twin Lakes, Copper Ridge):**
- Median assessed value: approximately $375,000 to $382,000
- 2027 non-school tax savings: approximately $950 to $1,050 per year
- 2028 non-school tax savings: approximately $1,900 to $2,100 per year (once exemption reaches $250,000)

**Valrico 33596 (Buckhorn, River Hills, Diamond Hill, Newsome zone):**
- Median assessed value: approximately $441,000 to $472,000
- 2027 non-school tax savings: approximately $1,100 to $1,200 per year
- 2028 non-school tax savings: approximately $2,100 to $2,400 per year

These savings do not appear on your November 2026 tax bill, which reflects current law. The first year of savings shows up in your November 2027 Hillsborough County tax notice. Use the [Hillsborough County Property Appraiser's homestead exemption portal](https://www.hcpafl.org/Property-Info/Exemptions-and-Classifications) to verify your assessed value and confirm your existing homestead exemption status.

## The Local Government Workaround Problem -- and Why the Special Session Matters

This is the detail many Valrico homeowners are not thinking about, and it may be the most consequential part of the special session.

Hillsborough County government relies heavily on property tax revenue to fund services that Valrico residents use every day: fire rescue, the HART bus network, library branches, parks maintenance, and capital infrastructure. When Amendment 3 reduces the taxable base for non-school purposes, it creates a revenue gap at the county level.

Counties and municipalities in Florida have several mechanisms to respond:

- **Increase the millage rate** (within constitutional limits) to maintain dollar revenue
- **Increase non-ad-valorem assessments** -- special assessments tied to specific services that are not subject to the homestead exemption rules
- **Reduce services** (politically difficult)
- **Issue debt** (long-term cost with interest)

The specific language of the special session legislation will determine how effectively local governments can use these mechanisms. If the session limits millage rate increases and constrains special assessment expansion, Valrico homeowners get to keep most or all of the Amendment 3 savings. If the session language is weak, some savings will be offset over time.

Watch for the [Tampa Bay Times legislative coverage](https://www.tampabay.com/florida-politics/) and official Florida Legislature updates as the session details are announced. The session is expected before the end of November or in early December 2026.

## Governor Transition: What Byron Donalds Means for Florida Housing Policy

Byron Donalds takes office January 5, 2027 as Florida's new governor. His housing and property tax positions will shape the 2027 legislative session, which is the first full session after Amendment 3 implementation.

The 2026 legislative session was widely seen as a disappointment on housing. Florida housing affordability groups reported that several high-profile housing supply bills failed to pass during the regular session, and affordability remained the top voter concern entering the midterms. [News4Jax reporting on the 2026 session](https://www.news4jax.com/news/local/2026/03/19/its-just-really-difficult-florida-lawmakers-struggle-to-deliver-affordability-relief-as-key-housing-bills-fail/) described the housing outcome as "just really difficult" for lawmakers.

What the new administration is likely to focus on:

**Zoning and permitting reform.** Donalds, a free-market conservative, has previously called for reductions in local government barriers to housing construction. Tampa Bay's housing shortfall exceeds 150,000 units according to a 2026 regional study, and state preemption of exclusionary zoning rules is one of the few tools with meaningful supply-side impact. Expect continued pressure on municipalities to allow more by-right construction, particularly multifamily near transit.

**Affordable housing funding continuity.** The 2026-2027 state budget preserved $165.7 million for SHIP (State Housing Initiatives Partnership), which provides flexible housing funding to Hillsborough County and other local governments. SHIP funds can be used for down payment assistance, rehabilitation loans, and infrastructure for affordable housing projects. See the [Florida Housing Finance Corporation SHIP program page](https://floridahousing.org/programs/homebuyer-overview-page/ship) for program details available through Hillsborough County.

**Property tax further reform.** The amendment's 2028 phase-up to $250,000 is automatic under the constitutional language, but Donalds may pursue additional property tax relief through the legislature -- potentially extending the concept to non-homestead properties more aggressively.

## Tampa Bay's Housing Shortfall: The Policy Context

No amount of property tax relief addresses the fundamental supply constraint in Tampa Bay's housing market. A 2026 regional study estimated the Tampa Bay area is short more than 150,000 housing units relative to current and projected population. That shortfall is the structural driver keeping prices elevated in Valrico, Brandon, Riverview, and the broader east Hillsborough market.

The post-election political landscape in Tampa Bay reflects housing cost pressure as a voter concern. In Florida House District 87, Democrat Emily Gregory defeated the Trump-endorsed Republican incumbent by running explicitly on housing cost relief -- a rare single-issue win in a district Trump carried by 10 points in 2024. See [International Business Times coverage of the Gregory race](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/emily-gregory-flips-trump-district-cost-living-1788101).

The Tampa-area Senate District 14 race was separated by just 408 votes in unofficial results as of November 8, with Democrat Brian Nathan leading. That race reflects the same affordability-driven frustration that Gregory ran on.

What this political environment means for Valrico and east Hillsborough in practical terms:

- **State intervention in local zoning** is more likely under a Donalds administration than a DeSantis-style ideological gridlock with the legislature
- **SHIP funding is bipartisan** and unlikely to be cut regardless of which party controls the legislature
- **The housing supply gap will not close in one or two years** -- Valrico's structural demand advantage, rooted in Newsome High School zoning and I-75 access, persists regardless of state policy direction
- **Investors should watch Hillsborough County's response to Amendment 3** -- if the county raises millage rates to offset revenue loss, that affects cap rates on investment properties and rental income projections

## What Homeowners and Buyers Should Do Before Year-End

**Homeowners already in Valrico:** You do not need to file anything for Amendment 3. If you have an active homestead exemption with the Hillsborough County Property Appraiser, you automatically receive the expanded exemption starting with your 2027 tax year bill. Confirm your exemption is on file at the [Hillsborough County Property Appraiser homestead portal](https://www.hcpafl.org/Property-Info/Exemptions-and-Classifications). If you are missing it, the deadline to file for the 2027 tax year is March 1, 2027.

**Sellers considering listing before year-end:** The special session limiting local government workarounds, if passed before January 1, makes Amendment 3 savings more certain and marketable to buyers. Use the confirmed savings in your listing marketing. A buyer comparing your Valrico home to a Riverview property outside Hillsborough County will find the tax picture meaningfully different. For listing strategy, see [how to sell a Valrico home in a 7 percent rate environment](/blog/how-to-sell-valrico-fl-home-at-7-percent-mortgage-rates-october-2026/).

**Buyers targeting a December close:** If you close by December 31, 2026, you establish homestead eligibility for the 2027 tax year and capture the first year of Amendment 3 savings. That is approximately $1,000 to $1,200 per year on a typical Valrico or Brandon home in the $400,000 to $450,000 range. Combined with current seller concession availability -- approximately 2% to 3% of purchase price in closing cost credits -- the December close argument is unusually strong. See the [year-end buyer action plan](/blog/valrico-fl-year-end-buyer-action-plan-close-before-december-31-2026/) for the full timeline.

**Investors holding Hillsborough County rental properties:** Watch the special session language on local government fee increases. If Hillsborough County responds to the revenue gap with expanded special assessments on rental properties or increased non-homestead millage, your carrying costs change. The non-homestead assessment growth cap reduction from 10% to 5% (also in Amendment 3) is beneficial for investment properties, but the local government response could offset part of that benefit. Run your cap rate projections under both a "workaround-limited" and "workaround-open" scenario until the session language is final. See the [Valrico FL investment property guide](/blog/valrico-fl-investment-property-cap-rates-cash-flow-q4-2026/) for current cap rate benchmarks.

## The Bottom Line

The special session DeSantis called is not a minor administrative step. It is the mechanism that determines how much of Amendment 3's constitutional tax savings actually reaches Valrico homeowners. The difference between a session with strong local government restrictions and a weak one is potentially hundreds of dollars per year in tax savings over the next decade.

The broader political transition -- from DeSantis to Donalds, from a deadlocked legislature to a new generation of leadership -- sets up 2027 as a consequential year for Florida housing policy. The fundamentals in Valrico and east Hillsborough County are strong: school zone demand, I-75 access, and new construction pipeline discipline all support stability. Policy tailwinds from Amendment 3 and potential zoning reform add to the medium-term case.
