## What "New Tampa" Actually Is

New Tampa is not a city. It is an unincorporated community in the northeast corner of Hillsborough County, within Tampa city limits, covering approximately 25 square miles north of Interstate 75 and Bruce B. Downs Boulevard. It was developed beginning in the 1990s as a series of master-planned communities, and the "New" in the name was marketing from the start -- a designation to distinguish the northeast suburban growth corridor from established east Tampa and Brandon.

The New Tampa area is defined by the 33647 ZIP code and includes ten large planned unit developments: Arbor Greene, K-Bar Ranch, Hampton Preserve, Hunter's Green, Heritage Isle, Cory Lake Isles, Tampa Palms, Basset Creek Estates, Esplanade of Tampa, and Grand Hampton. Each community has its own HOA, amenity package, and price tier. When buyers or sellers reference "New Tampa," they are almost always talking about one of these planned communities rather than the ZIP code as a whole.

## Neighborhood-by-Neighborhood Pricing

Not all of New Tampa prices the same. Here is what each primary community is delivering in 2026:

**Hunter's Green:** A gated golf course community developed in the 1990s. As of June 2026, 16 homes were active with a median list price of approximately $482,000 and an average price per square foot of $258. Hunter's Green is one of the more sought-after addresses in New Tampa due to its golf course amenity and gated access. The Hunter's Green Elementary feeder school carries strong ratings in Hillsborough County.

**Arbor Greene:** A premium community at the upper end of the New Tampa market. As of May 2026, 22 active and pending listings carried a median list price of approximately $652,000 at an average of $217 per square foot. Recent closed sales showed a median near $531,000 at $202 per square foot, reflecting the now-familiar gap between seller optimism and buyer-supported values. Arbor Greene targets the upper-tier buyer in New Tampa.

**K-Bar Ranch:** One of the newer developments in the 33647 ZIP code. K-Bar Ranch offers more affordable entry points than Hunter's Green and Arbor Greene, with smaller floor plans and modern construction from the 2010s. This is where buyers who want new or near-new construction in the mid-$400,000s find the most inventory in New Tampa.

**Cory Lake Isles:** A guard-gated community centered on Cory Lake, the largest lake within Tampa city limits. Waterfront properties command a significant premium here. This is not a market for buyers with a $450,000 budget -- expect to start in the $600,000 to $700,000 range for lakefront or canal-access homes, with the premium positions approaching seven figures.

**Tampa Palms:** One of the original New Tampa developments, located adjacent to the University of South Florida campus. Tampa Palms homes tend to be older than the rest of New Tampa (many built in the late 1980s and early 1990s), which creates both value and maintenance considerations. Entry prices here run $420,000 to $500,000 for single-family homes, with some townhomes and villas available at lower price points.

**Grand Hampton and Hampton Preserve:** Amenity-focused communities in the northern corridor of New Tampa. Resort-style pools, fitness centers, and tennis courts are standard. Pricing in the $480,000 to $600,000 range depending on size and view.

**Heritage Isle:** A 55-plus active adult community within New Tampa, serving buyers who want the area's amenity infrastructure and location in an age-restricted setting.

## The School Zone Reality in New Tampa

School zone is where New Tampa diverges most sharply from Valrico's 33596 ZIP code.

New Tampa's primary high school is Paul R. Wharton High School, located at 20150 Bruce B. Downs Boulevard. Wharton carries a B grade on Niche and a student-teacher ratio of 24 to 1. State test score data shows approximately 37% of students at proficiency in math and 46% in reading. Graduation rate is 85%. Average SAT score is approximately 1,150.

By comparison, Newsome High School in Valrico's 33596 ZIP consistently earns an A or A+ Niche rating, ranks among the top 10 to 15 high schools in Florida, and carries math and reading proficiency rates significantly above the state and county average. The Newsome zone is the single most powerful price driver in east Hillsborough County, producing a $60,000 to $150,000 premium over comparable homes in other submarkets.

Buyers who rank school zone performance as a primary criterion should weigh this carefully. A home in New Tampa at $470,000 is accessing a Wharton B-rated zone. A home in Valrico's 33596 at a similar or lower price is accessing Newsome's A-plus-rated zone. For families with children approaching high school age, this difference is not minor.

Elementary school quality in New Tampa is stronger: Hunters Green Elementary and other feeder schools in the community are well-regarded within Hillsborough County. The zone performance gap concentrates at the high school level.

## Days on Market and Negotiating Leverage

With a 55-day average at the ZIP level and 4.1% year-over-year price declines, New Tampa buyers have genuine negotiating leverage in 2026 -- more than they have had since 2019.

The practical impact:

**Seller concessions are obtainable.** On homes that have been listed 45 or more days in the 33647 ZIP, requesting $8,000 to $15,000 in closing cost credits is reasonable and frequently granted. Sellers who priced at spring 2022 expectations and have been sitting since spring 2026 are motivated to negotiate.

**Price reductions are happening.** Homes initially listed at $490,000 to $530,000 in Hunter's Green and similar communities are repricing to $465,000 to $505,000 after 60 to 90 days. Buyers who watch specific listings and make their move after the first price reduction often capture significant value.

**Inspection leverage is real.** New Tampa's housing stock skews toward construction from the 1990s and early 2000s, meaning roofs, HVAC systems, and exterior materials are reaching the end of their expected lifespans. A full inspection is not optional -- it is a financial necessity. Issues that sellers ignored during the seller's market of 2021 and 2022 are now negotiating points.

**CDD fees require due diligence.** Several New Tampa communities carry Community Development District fees ranging from $1,200 to $2,800 per year added to the property tax bill. These are separate from HOA fees and are frequently missed by buyers who focus only on the list price. Always ask your agent to pull the tax history so you see the full carrying cost before making an offer.

## Investment Potential in New Tampa

New Tampa has a mixed track record as a rental market because the communities are HOA-intensive. Many New Tampa HOAs either restrict or regulate rental activity, and some communities require minimum lease terms of 12 months, which limits short-term options.

For buy-and-hold investors who clear the HOA hurdles:

A $450,000 single-family home in New Tampa rents for approximately $2,400 to $2,800 per month in 2026, depending on community, size, and condition. That produces a gross yield of approximately 6.4% to 7.5% before expenses. After county taxes ($7,500 to $9,500/yr), homeowner's insurance ($2,800 to $4,500/yr), HOA fees ($100 to $350/month), any CDD obligation, and property management, net cap rates land in the 3.5% to 4.8% range.

That yield math is lower than the investor returns available in Ruskin (7.5%+ gross) or Plant City (7.7%+ gross) but delivers in a market with lower vacancy risk due to USF and medical corridor employment demand. New Tampa's tenant profile skews toward dual-income professional households, which produces lower turnover and lower collection risk than some other Hillsborough County submarkets.

The primary investor caution is the HOA and CDD complexity. Confirm your HOA rental rules before placing an offer, and always verify whether there is an existing CDD obligation and when it is expected to retire.

## What to Expect at the Mortgage Level

Buyers in New Tampa are primarily financing with conventional 30-year fixed loans at current rates of approximately 6.65% to 6.75% as of late August 2026. Buyers financing above $832,750 -- the 2026 FHFA conforming loan limit -- enter jumbo territory at approximately 6.76% to 6.85%, which affects some Cory Lake Isles and Arbor Greene transactions.

On a $450,000 purchase with 20% down ($360,000 loan) at 6.75%, the principal and interest payment is approximately $2,336 per month. Add property taxes of $625 to $800 per month, homeowner's insurance of $300 to $450 per month, HOA fees of $100 to $350 per month, and any CDD obligation, and the all-in monthly carrying cost on a typical New Tampa home runs $3,400 to $4,200 per month.

Buyers who want to see how that payment compares to renting in the same area should run a 12-month break-even analysis with their agent. At 2026 rent levels in New Tampa ($2,400 to $2,800/month for comparable properties), ownership has a higher monthly carry but builds equity against a $450,000-plus asset in a community with consistent long-term demand.
