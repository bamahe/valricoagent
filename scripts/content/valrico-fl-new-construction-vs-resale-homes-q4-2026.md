## Why List Price Is Not Total Cost

Five cost factors distinguish new construction from resale in Valrico in a way that changes the final monthly payment materially.

### 1. Builder Incentives and Rate Buydowns

As of Q4 2026, national builders are using affiliated mortgage companies to offer rate buydowns and closing cost credits on 70 to 80 percent of new home sales. For Pulte in Tampa Bay, buyers using Pulte Mortgage can access rate buydowns that reduce the 30-year fixed to approximately 5.25 to 5.50 percent against a prevailing market rate near 6.5 percent. Closing cost credits from Tampa Bay builders in this environment range from $5,000 to $25,000 depending on the community, the floor plan, and the buyer's move-in timeline.

A $20,000 closing cost credit applied toward rate buydown points saves a buyer approximately $148 per month on a $420,000 purchase over 30 years. That is a real and meaningful number. The condition is non-negotiable: buyers must use the builder's preferred lender to access the full incentive. Buyers who secure independent financing typically lose most or all of the package.

For a detailed breakdown of how buyers are negotiating with sellers in the current resale market, see our guide to [buyer concessions and fall 2026 negotiations in Valrico](/blog/valrico-fl-buyer-concessions-and-negotiations-fall-2026/).

### 2. CDD Fees at Valri Forest

Valri Forest's Community Development District fee funds infrastructure the developer installed, including roads, stormwater systems, and landscaping. It adds approximately $1,800 to $2,400 per year to the homeowner's tax bill in perpetuity, or until the district's bonds retire in 15 to 30 years. At $2,100 per year that is $175 per month added to true ownership cost, without any mortgage balance offset.

Northwood Estates has no CDD fee. Many resale homes in Valrico's established subdivisions including Bloomingdale, Buckhorn, Twin Lakes, and Copper Ridge also carry no CDD fee. When comparing a $417,000 Pulte home at Valri Forest to a $380,000 resale in Bloomingdale, the monthly CDD obligation narrows the apparent price gap by approximately $175 before even accounting for the base price difference.

### 3. Tariff-Driven Construction Cost Premium

This is the newest factor affecting new home prices in 2026. The National Association of Home Builders estimates that [tariffs on imported building materials have added approximately $9,200 to the cost of a typical new home](https://www.nahb.org/blog/2025/03/canada-mexico-tariffs-delayed-higher-lumber-prices-still-likely). Iron and steel prices are up approximately 14 to 17.9 percent year-over-year as of mid-2026 from Section 232 tariffs. Softwood lumber, more than 70 percent of which is imported from Canada, is up approximately 6 to 10 percent. Gypsum for drywall has been less affected.

Large national builders like Pulte absorb some of this cost and pass the rest into base prices and reduced upgrade allowances. A resale home priced in Q4 2026 already reflects 2022 or 2023-era construction costs, the period when that specific home was built. A new home priced in late 2026 incorporates 2026 material prices. For a buyer comparing a $417,000 Pulte home to a $375,000 resale in the same school zone, some meaningful portion of that $42,000 spread represents tariff-driven cost escalation that simply did not exist three years ago.

### 4. Resale Negotiating Leverage

Resale sellers in Valrico are negotiating. The 96.5 to 97.5 percent sale-to-list ratio means a $382,000 list price is typically closing at approximately $369,000 to $373,000. On top of the price reduction, motivated sellers in this market are offering closing cost credits of $5,000 to $10,000, rate buydown contributions of $3,000 to $6,000, and repair credits from inspection. A seller with a home that has been on the market 90 days may concede $15,000 to $20,000 in total value from a $380,000 ask.

Builders rarely match this flexibility. Builder incentives are structured, lender-specific, and time-limited. Resale negotiation is messier but can generate comparable or greater buyer savings, particularly in the $350,000 to $420,000 price band where resale inventory in 33594 is deepest right now.

### 5. Upgrades and What Base Price Actually Buys

New construction base prices at Valri Forest and Northwood Estates do not include upgrades. A buyer who closes on the base floor plan receives builder-grade finishes including standard cabinets, standard countertops, and standard flooring. Moving to quartz countertops, upgraded tile, luxury vinyl plank flooring, and an extended covered lanai typically adds $20,000 to $50,000 at the design center, depending on selection. Many buyers discover the full scope of this only after going under contract.

Resale homes in Valrico's 2015 to 2022 build range have typically already been upgraded once by a prior owner. Granite or quartz countertops, hardwood or premium LVP flooring, and updated fixtures are standard in most Bloomingdale homes listed at $370,000 to $420,000. The buyer gets a finished product without the design center premium. ## Financing Timeline and Rate Lock Risk

New construction buyers in Valrico face a financing challenge that resale buyers do not: rate lock expiration on extended timelines. If a home is six to nine months from delivery, a buyer who locks a rate at contract will almost certainly see that lock expire before closing. Extending rate locks past 60 days typically costs 0.125 to 0.25 percent in added rate or upfront fees.

A buyer purchasing a new construction home in Q4 2026 for a spring or summer 2027 delivery is essentially taking a view on where mortgage rates will be six to nine months from now. If rates decline, they benefit. If rates hold or rise, they absorb the increase. Resale buyers in Valrico close in 30 to 60 days, well within standard lock windows. For buyers who need rate certainty, resale removes the lock expiration risk entirely.

## Which Type of Home Is Right for Which Buyer

**New construction makes the more compelling case if:**
- You can use the builder's preferred lender and qualify for the full rate buydown and closing cost incentive package
- You have flexibility on move-in timeline and can accommodate six to nine months from contract to closing
- You place a high priority on a new-home warranty covering all systems for one to two years
- You need Newsome zone access and WestBay's floor plan design meets your space requirements
- You have the design center budget to move beyond the base floor plan finishes

**Resale makes the stronger case if:**
- You need to close within 30 to 60 days due to job relocation, lease expiration, or school-year timing
- You want to avoid CDD fees entirely and your target resale neighborhood carries none
- You have a buyer's agent with current leverage experience who can negotiate price reductions plus seller credits plus a rate buydown contribution from one seller
- Your price target is in the $360,000 to $420,000 range in 33594, where resale inventory is currently the deepest in the market
- You want a home with mature landscaping, established neighborhoods, and upgraded finishes already in place

**The core tradeoff:** A builder's rate buydown at 5.25 to 5.50 percent on a $420,000 purchase saves meaningful money compared to a 6.5 percent market rate loan. But a resale buyer who negotiates a $15,000 price reduction plus $8,000 in seller credits on a $380,000 house ends up with a materially lower loan balance and comparable or better total cost.
