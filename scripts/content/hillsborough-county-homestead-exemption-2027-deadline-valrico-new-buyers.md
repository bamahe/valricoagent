## Why This Matters More in 2027 Than Any Prior Year

Florida voters passed Amendment 3 on November 3, 2026, with more than 60% approval. That constitutional amendment expands the homestead exemption significantly starting January 1, 2027. For 2026 buyers who file on time, the financial benefit is larger than it has ever been.

Here is the specific dollar impact for a Valrico home with a $415,000 assessed value:

**Before Amendment 3 (prior years):**
- Base homestead exemption: $25,000
- Second homestead exemption (non-school millage only): $25,000
- Total reduction in taxable value for school district purposes: $25,000
- Total reduction for non-school purposes: $50,000
- Estimated annual tax savings: $550 to $700

**With Amendment 3 (2027 tax year):**
- Base homestead exemption: $25,000 (unchanged)
- Non-school expanded exemption: increased to $150,000 (up from $25,000)
- Total reduction in taxable value for school district purposes: $25,000
- Total reduction for non-school millage purposes: $175,000
- Estimated annual tax savings on a $415,000 home: approximately $1,100 to $1,250

That is a meaningful improvement. And it compounds through the Save Our Homes cap, which limits assessed value increases to 3% per year once your homestead exemption is in place. The longer your exemption runs, the more valuable the cap becomes as market values rise.

For a buyer who waits one year and misses the 2027 filing deadline, the cost is not just the annual savings. It is the entire year of protection from assessed value creep, which starts the moment your exemption goes into effect.

## Who Is Eligible

To qualify for the Florida homestead exemption on your 2027 tax bill, you must meet all of the following requirements:

**You must own the property.** The home must be titled in your name or in a trust you control. If you bought in 2026 and your deed is recorded with the Hillsborough County Clerk of Courts, you meet this requirement.

**The property must be your primary residence as of January 1, 2027.** You do not have to have lived there for a full year. A buyer who closed on December 15, 2026 and moved in before January 1, 2027 is eligible for the 2027 exemption. The key date is January 1.

**You must be a Florida resident.** This means your Florida residence is your permanent legal domicile. You should have a Florida driver's license or state ID with your Valrico address, Florida voter registration at the address (if applicable), and your vehicles registered in Florida. You do not need to have all of these on the exact filing date, but they are what the Property Appraiser looks for.

**You must not claim homestead in another state.** Claiming primary residence benefits in another state disqualifies you from the Florida homestead exemption. This is a common issue for buyers who have recently relocated from another state and still have a primary residence designation, veterans' benefit, or homestead credit active there.

**The property must be a residential property used as your home.** Single-family residences, townhomes, condominiums, and manufactured homes all qualify as long as they meet the other requirements.

## How to File: The Step-by-Step Process

The Hillsborough County Property Appraiser's office offers three ways to file: online, in person, or by mail. Online is the fastest and most common.

### Online Filing at HCPAFL.org

The official homestead exemption e-file portal is at [hcpafl.org/E-Filing/Homestead-E-file](https://www.hcpafl.org/E-Filing/Homestead-E-file). The process takes approximately 10 to 15 minutes if you have your documents ready.

You will need:
- Your property parcel number (found on your closing documents or your most recent tax bill)
- Your Florida driver's license or ID number, with the address matching your new home
- Your Social Security number
- The legal description of the property (on your deed)
- If you owned a previous Florida home with homestead exemption, the address of that prior property for portability calculation

The online system will confirm your submission by email. Keep that confirmation. If you do not receive it within 24 hours, follow up by phone at 813-272-6100.

### In Person

The Hillsborough County Property Appraiser has four office locations. The main office is at:

Hillsborough County Property Appraiser
601 E. Kennedy Blvd., 16th Floor
Tampa, FL 33602

Bring your Florida driver's license or ID, your deed or closing disclosure, and your Social Security number. Office hours are typically Monday through Friday, 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM. No appointment is required, but wait times are longer in January and February as the March 1 deadline approaches. Filing in November or December avoids the rush.

### By Mail

Download Form DR-501 from the Hillsborough County Property Appraiser's website, complete it, and mail it to the main office address above with copies of your supporting documentation. Mail filing is reliable but slower. If you file by mail, do so early enough to arrive well before March 1.

## The March 1, 2027 Deadline: What Happens If You Miss It

The statutory deadline to file is March 1 of the tax year for which you want the exemption. Miss March 1, 2027 and you will not receive the homestead exemption for the 2027 tax year. Your tax bill will be calculated without the exemption and will arrive in November 2027 at full taxable value.

Florida law allows for a late-filing window. The Property Appraiser may accept applications up to 25 days after the mailing of the annual Notice of Proposed Property Taxes, which is typically mailed in August of each year. However, this late window is not guaranteed and is subject to the Property Appraiser's discretion. Do not plan around the late window.

If you miss both deadlines, you will need to wait and refile for the 2028 tax year. You cannot retroactively apply the exemption to a year you missed.

## Save Our Homes: The Long-Term Value of Filing Early

Homestead exemption does two things. First, it reduces your taxable value by the exemption amount in the year you file. Second, it starts the Save Our Homes clock.

Save Our Homes limits the annual increase in your property's assessed value to 3% per year or the Consumer Price Index, whichever is lower. Without homestead exemption, your property is re-assessed at market value annually, with no cap. In a market like Valrico, where values can move 5% to 8% per year in a strong cycle, the Save Our Homes cap compresses your tax growth substantially over time.

Consider the difference over 10 years on a home bought at $415,000 assessed value in 2027:

**Without homestead exemption (re-assessed at market each year, assuming 4% annual appreciation):**
- Year 10 assessed value: approximately $592,000
- Year 10 tax bill at 20 mills: approximately $11,840 per year

**With homestead exemption and Save Our Homes cap at 3% per year:**
- Year 10 assessed value (capped): approximately $521,000
- Year 10 taxable value after exemptions: approximately $371,000
- Year 10 tax bill at 20 mills: approximately $7,420 per year
- Annual savings versus no exemption: approximately $4,420

The cap does not kick in until your homestead exemption is active. Every year you delay filing is a year the Save Our Homes protection does not apply.

## Portability: What Happens When You Move Again

Florida's homestead portability provision allows you to transfer your accumulated Save Our Homes differential to a new Florida home when you sell and buy another primary residence. The differential is the difference between your property's just market value and its capped assessed value at the time of sale.

As of 2026, the portability cap was $500,000. HJR 211, which appeared on the same November 3 ballot as Amendment 3, would have removed that cap but required 60% approval and results are still pending at time of publication. Even at the current $500,000 cap, most Valrico homeowners will not accumulate a differential large enough to be constrained by it for many years.

Portability must be applied for when you file homestead on your new property. It is not automatic. If you previously owned a Florida home with homestead exemption, bring documentation of your prior home's assessed value and just market value to the application.

## What Valrico Homeowners Pay in Property Taxes

Understanding the full picture of property taxes in Valrico and Hillsborough County helps you benchmark your expected savings.

Hillsborough County applies several separate millage rates that combine to form your total tax bill. The major components include:

**Hillsborough County general operating millage:** Approximately 5.0 mills
**Hillsborough County School Board:** Approximately 7.0 mills (required state levy plus discretionary)
**Children's Board levy:** Approximately 0.5 mills
**Other special districts:** Varies by location, typically 0.3 to 1.5 mills

The combined millage for most of Valrico is approximately 19 to 21 mills, depending on your specific location and applicable special districts. No CDD fees apply to most established Valrico neighborhoods, though some newer developments carry them.

**Sample 2027 tax bill calculation for a 33596 home with $465,000 assessed value with Amendment 3 homestead exemption:**

- School board taxable value: $465,000 minus $25,000 base exemption = $440,000
- Non-school taxable value: $465,000 minus $175,000 (base + expanded non-school) = $290,000
- School board portion (7.0 mills): $440,000 x 0.007 = $3,080
- Non-school portion (12.5 mills): $290,000 x 0.0125 = $3,625
- Estimated total tax bill: approximately $6,705

Without the homestead exemption, the same home at $465,000 assessed value would generate approximately:
- $465,000 x 0.0195 = $9,068

Annual savings: approximately $2,363.

## Frequently Asked Questions from Valrico Buyers

**Do I need to refile every year once my homestead exemption is in place?**
No. The homestead exemption automatically renews each year as long as your primary residence and eligibility status remain the same. You do not refile unless you move or your circumstances change.

**I closed in November 2026. Can I still get the exemption for the 2027 tax year?**
Yes, as long as your property is your primary residence as of January 1, 2027 and you file before March 1, 2027. Closing date does not matter as much as where you are living on January 1.

**My closing documents show the prior owner's address. What do I use for my Florida ID?**
Update your Florida driver's license or ID to show your new Valrico address before filing. The Property Appraiser needs the ID address to match the property address. The DMV allows address changes online at Florida DMV's myflhsmv.gov portal.

**What if the assessed value on the property card does not match what I paid?**
Assessed value and sale price are related but not the same. The Property Appraiser re-assesses properties after sale using the sale price as a benchmark, so your first full year after closing often reflects your purchase price closely. If you believe the assessment is incorrect, you have the right to file a Value Adjustment Board petition. The deadline for that is September 15 of each year.

**Can a trust or LLC hold the property and still qualify for homestead?**
A revocable living trust where the beneficiary lives in the home as their primary residence typically qualifies. An LLC or irrevocable trust generally does not qualify. If your property is held in a trust, verify eligibility with the Property Appraiser before filing.
