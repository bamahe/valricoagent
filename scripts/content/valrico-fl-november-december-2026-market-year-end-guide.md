## The November 3 Ballot: What Every Buyer and Seller Needs to Understand Before It Votes

The November 3, 2026 Florida ballot includes two constitutional amendments with direct financial implications for Valrico homeowners. Most buyers and sellers are unaware of the math involved.

### Amendment 3: Expanding the Homestead Exemption

Amendment 3 would increase the non-school homestead property tax exemption from $25,000 (where it has sat since 1995) to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028, with inflation adjustments beginning in 2029. It requires 60% voter approval to pass.

For a Valrico homeowner with a $415,000 assessed value, passage means:

**Under current law (if Amendment 3 fails):**
Taxable value for non-school purposes: $415,000 minus $50,000 (base + second exemption) = $365,000. Combined millage of approximately 11.0 mills (non-school) on $365,000 = approximately $4,015 per year.

**Under Amendment 3 (2027, if it passes):**
Taxable value for non-school purposes: $415,000 minus $150,000 = $265,000. Same millage on $265,000 = approximately $2,915 per year. **Annual savings: approximately $1,100.**

A buyer who closes before December 31, 2026 and establishes homestead exemption is eligible to receive this benefit for the 2027 tax year. A buyer who waits until January or later gets the same benefit, but loses the first full year of savings. At a $1,100 annual savings, the net present value of that first year alone is real money, particularly when current mortgage rates are making every monthly cost line count.

### Save Our Homes Portability Cap Removal

HJR 211 on the same ballot would remove the current $500,000 cap on Save Our Homes (SOH) portability transfers. Currently, Florida homeowners can transfer up to $500,000 of their accumulated SOH differential to a new Florida home when they move. If passed, there would be no cap.

This change primarily affects high-value homeowners who have accumulated SOH differentials above $500,000, most commonly sellers in River Hills Country Club or Diamond Hill who bought at 2005 to 2015 prices and watched assessed values rise dramatically. For a River Hills seller with a $600,000 assessed value and $150,000 of accumulated SOH savings, the cap is not the binding constraint. But for River Hills sellers with homes worth $800,000 to $1.2 million and long ownership histories, the portability cap has been a material financial consideration when evaluating a move. Removing it makes moving more financially viable and could modestly unlock supply in the upper tier.

**Practical implication for November buyers:** Do not structure your closing date around the ballot. Either outcome is workable. If Amendment 3 passes, your homestead exemption automatically captures the benefit for 2027 with no additional action. If it fails, your tax bill continues under existing law. The savings from a correctly structured offer or concession in a November closing will almost certainly exceed any ballot-outcome-dependent delay.

## Why November and December Are Better for Buyers Than Most Expect

The holiday slowdown narrative about real estate is real in some markets and overstated in others. In Valrico, the dynamics favor buyers who act in November and December for several compounding reasons.

**Fewer competing buyers.** November and December see a 30 to 40% reduction in new buyer inquiries compared to September and October. Buyers who are serious about closing before year-end are still active; weekend-warrior explorers are not. This means your offer has less competition even on well-priced properties.

**Sellers who are still listed are motivated.** A seller who has been on the market since July or August and is still listed in November has already demonstrated patience, but their motivation to close before the holidays, before the end of the tax year, or before carrying another month of ownership costs is real. Sellers who are not motivated have already pulled their listings. The homes you are touring in November are disproportionately from sellers who genuinely need to close.

**Concessions are the norm.** Based on active Valrico listing data, approximately 35 to 37% of listings have taken at least one price reduction. In November and December, seller-paid closing costs of $8,000 to $12,000 are common on homes above $400,000. Rate buydowns, prepaid HOA, and home warranty contributions are standard asks that most November sellers will accommodate.

**January brings fresh competition.** New listings historically spike in January and February as sellers who waited through the holidays enter the market, and spring buyers who were prequalified in fall begin touring. A buyer who closes in November or December enters 2027 with equity, a homestead exemption in place for the new tax year, and no competition from January's influx of new-buyer activity.

## Buyer Strategy: November and December 2026

**Step 1: Know your numbers before you tour.** At 6.75% on a $380,000 loan (20% down on a $475,000 purchase), principal and interest is approximately $2,467 per month. Property taxes, insurance, and HOA add $700 to $1,000 per month depending on the property. Total housing cost in 33596 at this price runs $3,200 to $3,500 per month.

**Step 2: Target the right DOM window.** Homes listed in the last 21 days in Valrico are typically at first-offering prices. Homes at 45 to 75 days on market are in the leverage window: sellers have already done at least one reduction and are psychologically prepared to negotiate. Homes over 75 days on market require due diligence on why they have not sold, but often yield the best concession packages.

**Step 3: Use closed comps from October and November, not spring 2026.** Spring 2026 sale prices were 3 to 5% higher than fall 2026 in most Valrico segments. An offer anchored to October closed comps is defensible. An offer anchored to spring comps overpays.

**Step 4: Ask for closing cost contributions, not just price reductions.** A $10,000 price reduction on a 30-year loan saves approximately $56 per month. A $10,000 seller-paid closing cost contribution saves $10,000 today, at the moment of highest financial stress (the closing). Both cost the seller the same. The closing cost contribution is almost always the better structure for the buyer.

**Step 5: Lock your rate before November 3.** If you are in contract or close to it before the ballot, consider locking your rate. Rate volatility around elections is normal. A lock gives you certainty.

## Seller Strategy: November and December 2026

Sellers who list in November are swimming against the seasonal current. That does not mean you cannot sell. It means you have to be precise about execution.

**Price to the current market, not the market you remember.** The most common seller mistake in Q4 is anchoring to spring 2026 prices. Fall 2026 comps in 33594 are running $6,000 to $15,000 below comparable spring 2026 sales. Pricing to that spring number means you are already 2 to 4% above the market before you open the door for showings.

**Invest in your presentation.** Professional photography, a clean interior, and decluttered rooms matter more in November and December than in any other season. Buyers touring in November are largely doing so on weekends and weeknights after dark. If your home does not photograph well, you are invisible to the 90%+ of buyers who start online.

**Set a hard deadline.** If you need to close by December 31 for a tax, relocation, or estate reason, communicate that flexibility explicitly to buyers' agents. A November seller who genuinely needs a quick close can attract buyers who want possession before year-end. The motivated timeline on your side becomes an asset rather than a weakness when it is framed correctly.

**Consider a Q1 2027 list date instead.** If you are not under time pressure, the math often favors waiting for January or February 2027. Spring season begins February 15 in Valrico. Rates may be modestly lower. More buyers are active. If Amendment 3 passes in November, the lower tax outlook improves buyer purchasing power. Sellers who wait for spring 2027 should begin pre-listing preparation now: paint, updates, and professional staging take 8 to 12 weeks to execute properly.

The 2027 Valrico market will be shaped primarily by mortgage rate movement. The Fed's easing cycle, which began in late 2024, has been more gradual than buyers hoped. The current rate environment (6.75% to 7.00%) is likely to ease modestly toward the 6.25% to 6.50% range by mid-2027 based on Fannie Mae and Mortgage Bankers Association consensus forecasts.

At 6.25%, the monthly principal and interest payment on a $380,000 loan drops from approximately $2,467 (at 6.75%) to approximately $2,340, a difference of $127 per month. That $127 per month improvement, combined with the potential Amendment 3 property tax savings of $80 to $120 per month, could increase effective buyer purchasing power by $200 to $250 per month entering spring 2027. That purchasing power unlocks buyers who are currently just out of reach on their qualification or budget.

For Valrico specifically, the base case for 2027 appreciation is 2 to 4% for the combined market, with 33596 likely performing at the higher end of that range due to persistent Newsome zone demand. The downside scenario (rates above 7% through 2027 and a broader economic softening) produces flat to mildly negative outcomes. The upside scenario (rates below 6% and Amendment 3 passage) produces 4 to 6% appreciation and a return to 30-day DOM conditions in core 33596.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I buy in Valrico before the November 3 ballot or wait to see how Amendment 3 votes?**
Buying before the ballot gives you the option to benefit from Amendment 3 in 2027 if it passes, with no downside if it fails. Waiting until after the ballot to see the outcome means you miss the year-end buying window, face January competition from new listings, and potentially pay higher prices in spring 2027 if Amendment 3 passage drives more buyer demand. The ballot should not delay a purchase decision; the purchase decision should be made on the fundamentals of price and affordability today.

**What is the best way to negotiate a Valrico home purchase in November 2026?**
Focus your negotiation on seller-paid closing cost contributions rather than price reductions. Ask for $8,000 to $12,000 in closing cost credits on homes above $400,000, particularly homes that have been listed more than 45 days. Pair that with a rate buydown request to lower your first-year payment. Price reductions help only modestly on a monthly payment basis; closing cost contributions and buydowns reduce your out-of-pocket at the moment it matters most.

**Is Valrico holding value better than the rest of Hillsborough County in fall 2026?**
Yes. Hillsborough County is down approximately 3 to 6% year-over-year on median sale prices depending on the source. Valrico 33596 is flat to slightly positive. Valrico 33594 is down 1 to 2%. The Newsome High School zone is the primary driver of 33596's outperformance. Buyers who want a Hillsborough County suburb with defensible long-term value should look seriously at 33596 while prices are flat rather than waiting to see what 2027 brings.

**What happens to Valrico home prices if mortgage rates drop to 6.25% in 2027?**
A drop from 6.75% to 6.25% improves buyer monthly purchasing power by approximately $100 to $130 per month on a typical Valrico purchase. Historically in Hillsborough County, each 0.5% rate reduction unlocks an estimated 8 to 12% of previously sidelined buyers. In 33596 specifically, where the buyer pool includes school-zone-motivated buyers who are not purely rate-sensitive, a rate decline accelerates existing demand rather than creating new demand. The 33596 market is most likely to see the first sustained DOM compression of the new rate cycle.

**When is the best time to list a home in Valrico for 2027?**
The historical peak for Valrico listings activity is February 15 through April 30. Sellers who want maximum competition among buyers and the best chance for multiple offers should target a February or early March 2027 list date. That means beginning preparation, including paint, staging consultation, and professional photography, in November or December 2026. Waiting until February to start prep means listing in April, which is past peak activity.
