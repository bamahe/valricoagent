Florida voters approved Amendment 3 on November 3, 2026 by a 62 percent margin, clearing the 60 percent supermajority the state constitution requires. That vote changed the math for anyone still considering buying a home in Valrico. If you close on a Valrico FL home before December 31, 2026, you can file for homestead exemption by March 1, 2027 and capture the first year of expanded property tax savings starting January 1, 2027. If you close in January, you wait a full additional year.

## Why December 31 Is the Right Deadline

Florida homestead exemption requires you to own the property and have it as your primary residence as of January 1 of the tax year. To receive the Amendment 3 expanded exemption for the 2027 tax year, you must own the home and have established homestead as of January 1, 2027. That means closing by December 31, 2026.

The dollar savings are real. For a Valrico 33594 home with a $380,000 assessed value, Amendment 3 raises the non-school homestead exemption from $25,000 to $150,000. At Hillsborough County's combined non-school millage rate of approximately 7.57 mills, that additional $125,000 in exempt value saves you roughly $946 per year starting in 2027. For a 33596 home assessed at $469,000, the annual savings land closer to $1,100 to $1,200.

A buyer who closes December 15 captures those savings starting 2027. A buyer who closes January 15 waits until 2028. That gap represents $1,000 to $1,200 in real money.

## Step 1: Get Your Pre-Approval in Order Now

You cannot close by December 31 without a pre-approval that is current, complete, and in hand. If you are working from a pre-qualification letter or one that is more than 90 days old, restart the process this week.

A fully underwritten pre-approval from a lender who has verified your income, assets, and credit score takes two to five business days with a responsive borrower. Some lenders offer a TBD (to be determined) property approval, which means the underwriter reviews your file against a hypothetical property, so the only remaining condition at contract is an appraisal and title search. This is the strongest position a buyer can be in.

Current 30-year fixed mortgage rates as of early November 2026 are running approximately 6.3 to 6.75 percent. At 6.5 percent on a $380,000 loan with 20 percent down (purchase price of $475,000), the monthly principal and interest is approximately $2,403. That is the number to run against your budget before looking at homes.

One point often overlooked: if you are buying in Valrico with a VA loan, FHA loan, or conventional loan with less than 20 percent down, your lender's timeline may be three to four weeks from ratified contract to closing. Factor that into your December 31 target date. With a 30 to 35-day typical closing window in Hillsborough County, you need a contract in place by approximately November 25 to December 1.

## Step 2: Know Exactly What You Are Looking For

Buyers who close quickly are buyers who have already done the homework. The December window is not the time to discover that you need a pool or a three-car garage. Know before you shop.

In Valrico, the key decision points are ZIP code and school zone. The 33594 ZIP code (Bloomingdale, Copper Ridge, Twin Lakes) has a median sold price around $380,000 and feeds Bloomingdale High School. The 33596 ZIP (River Hills, Buckhorn, Diamond Hill, Brentwood Hills) has a median sold price near $469,000 and feeds both Newsome High School and Bloomingdale High depending on exact address. The school zone alone can add $50,000 to $90,000 to a comparable home's price.

Secondary decisions that drive the specific neighborhoods you search:
- HOA or no HOA (most Valrico neighborhoods have an HOA; homes without one tend to be on larger lots in older sections)
- Pool (pool homes carry a premium of $20,000 to $40,000 at comparable square footage)
- Lot size (Valrico lots run from 0.14 to 0.60 acres depending on neighborhood; River Hills and older Bloomingdale sections have the larger lots)
- Year built (Valrico's stock runs from early 1980s through 2024 new construction; roof age and HVAC age affect your insurance rate and near-term maintenance budget)

Knowing these criteria in advance means you can commit quickly when the right home appears, rather than hesitating through additional showings.

## Step 3: Set Up Automated Search Alerts

The Hillsborough County MLS (Stellar MLS) is the authoritative source of active listings. As of early November 2026, Valrico has approximately 246 to 260 active homes across both ZIP codes. That inventory gives buyers genuine selection without the panic-offer dynamics of 2021 and 2022.

Set up automated search alerts through your agent's portal or a reputable aggregator such as Realtor.com so you are notified within minutes when a home matching your criteria is listed or reduces its price. In November and December, motivated sellers who have been sitting on the market since summer will begin making meaningful price reductions. Those homes represent the best opportunities for a buyer who can close quickly.

Target homes that have been listed for 60 or more days. In the current Valrico market, with an average days on market of 45 to 71 days depending on price tier, a 60-day listing has typically already taken at least one price reduction and has a seller who understands the market reality.

## Step 4: Have Your Team in Place

The difference between a 25-day closing and a 45-day closing is often the buyer's team. You should have all of these arranged before making an offer.

**Lender:** Pre-approval in hand, responsive loan officer who answers weekends. Confirm that your lender can commit to a December 31 closing on a contract signed the first week of December. Some lenders will and some will not. Know before you offer.

**Inspector:** A licensed home inspector in Hillsborough County is typically available within five to seven business days of contract. Book one before you are under contract so you can confirm availability for your target window.

**Title company or closing attorney:** Florida is a title state. Your closing agent coordinates the title search, payoff figures, and closing disclosure. A reputable Hillsborough County title company can typically close in 21 to 30 days.

**Agent:** An agent with current active experience in Valrico 33594 and 33596 knows which sellers have already reduced, which HOAs have unusual restrictions or high delinquency rates, and which neighborhoods have known flood zone issues. That local knowledge compresses the discovery phase of your search.

## Step 5: Negotiate with December Leverage

The December market in Valrico favors buyers for two distinct reasons: fewer competing buyers and sellers who have been listed through summer and fall and want to close before year-end for their own tax reasons.

Do not let the urgency of the December 31 deadline shift negotiating power to the seller. You have leverage, too. Here is how to use it without overpaying:

**Price:** With a 33 to 42 percent price-reduction rate across Valrico's active inventory, start with the most recent reduced list price as your ceiling, not your opening. For a home listed at $450,000 that reduced from $475,000 60 days ago, the data-supported offer range is $430,000 to $445,000 depending on condition and comparable sales.

**Seller concessions:** Request seller-paid closing costs of two to three percent of the purchase price. At a $450,000 purchase, that is $9,000 to $13,500 applied toward your lender fees, prepaid insurance, and escrow setup. This is a standard request in the current Valrico market. Most motivated sellers accept.

**Rate buydown:** An alternative to price reduction is a seller-funded temporary or permanent rate buydown. A 2-1 buydown on a $380,000 loan at 6.5 percent costs approximately $7,600 and lowers your year-one rate to 4.5 percent and year-two rate to 5.5 percent, before resetting to 6.5 percent in year three. Many buyers prefer this structure to a price reduction because the monthly payment benefit is immediate.

**Inspection contingency:** Keep your inspection contingency. The December timeline is tight, but waiving inspection in Valrico is not a sound tradeoff. A five-day inspection period is standard and workable within a 30 to 35-day closing window.

## Step 6: Understand the Closing Timeline

A realistic December 31 closing timeline from initial offer works like this:

| Milestone | Target Date |
|---|---|
| Offer submitted and accepted | November 25 to December 1 |
| Inspection completed | December 5 to December 8 |
| Inspection repairs negotiated | December 10 |
| Appraisal ordered | December 1 (order same day as contract) |
| Appraisal completed | December 12 to December 18 |
| Clear to close issued | December 22 to December 26 |
| Closing date | December 29 to December 31 |

The pinch point is the appraisal. In Hillsborough County, appraisals in December are running 10 to 18 days from order to delivery. Work with a lender who orders the appraisal immediately upon contract and who has relationships with local certified appraisers. This one step separates buyers who close by December 31 from buyers who slip into January.

## Step 7: File Your Homestead Exemption by March 1

Closing by December 31 qualifies you for homestead, but you still need to file. The deadline to file for the 2027 tax year with the Hillsborough County Property Appraiser is March 1, 2027. File online at the Hillsborough County Property Appraiser's website (hcpafl.org) or in person at their offices.

Filing after March 1 means you lose the exemption for 2027 and cannot receive the Amendment 3 savings until 2028. Set a calendar reminder for February 15 and handle it then.

You will also want to file the Save Our Homes portability application (Form DR-501T) if you are transferring a homestead benefit from a prior Florida home. Same March 1 deadline, same portal.
