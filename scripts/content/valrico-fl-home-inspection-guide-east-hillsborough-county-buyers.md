## Why Florida Home Inspections Are Different

The combination of subtropical heat, year-round humidity, intense rain seasons, and a housing stock that skews toward the 20 to 40-year age range creates a specific set of risk categories that do not appear with the same frequency in other markets.

Florida homes work their mechanical systems hard. An air conditioner that might last 25 years in Minnesota lasts 12 to 15 years in Tampa Bay. A roof that qualifies for insurance in Ohio cannot get coverage in Hillsborough County once it passes 20 years. Moisture finds every gap in the building envelope because humidity is not seasonal -- it is constant.

The other major difference is the insurance connection. In Florida, what your inspector finds affects what you pay for homeowners insurance, and in some cases whether you can get coverage at all. A roof over 15 years old, a panel box flagged as a fire risk, or the absence of a wind mitigation credit can add $3,000 to $6,000 per year to your insurance premiums on a mid-range Valrico home. Getting those facts before closing is not optional.

## Valrico's Housing Stock: What Age Means for Buyers

Understanding when different Valrico neighborhoods were built helps buyers anticipate what they are likely to find.

**Bloomingdale (33594):** Most homes in Bloomingdale were built from the late 1980s through the early 2000s. That puts the typical home between 25 and 40 years old as of 2026. Homes in this range have often had one roof replacement and are approaching the second. Original HVAC systems have long since been replaced, but buyers may encounter second-generation systems that are themselves 10 to 15 years old. Plumbing in Bloomingdale's oldest sections occasionally includes polybutylene supply lines, which were installed from the late 1970s through 1995 before being discontinued due to failure rates.

**Buckhorn and Buckhorn Preserve (33596):** Development of the Buckhorn communities began in the early 1990s and extended through the mid-2000s, putting most homes at 20 to 35 years old. The roof situation is similar to Bloomingdale: many homes are on their first or second roof, and buyers at the lower end of this age range may be one major storm season from a required replacement. The older Buckhorn sections along the community's established streets have the most inspection exposure.

**River Hills Country Club (33596):** River Hills was developed from the early 1990s through the 2000s. Homes here are often larger and have more complex systems -- multiple HVAC zones, larger roofs, more square footage of tile -- which means more items for the inspector to cover and proportionally higher repair costs when something goes wrong.

**Diamond Hill (33596):** Diamond Hill's development era is similar to River Hills, with most homes built in the late 1990s and early 2000s. The community's larger lot sizes mean more exterior exposure: larger driveways, longer irrigation runs, and more landscape drainage to evaluate.

## The Standard Inspection: What It Covers and What It Doesn't

A Florida-licensed home inspector covers the structure, roof exterior, HVAC systems, plumbing, electrical, and visible interior surfaces including evidence of moisture intrusion. A standard inspection does NOT include swimming pool equipment, irrigation systems, well and septic systems, or a determination of whether termites or other wood-destroying organisms are present. Those require separate specialty inspections.

The average cost for a general home inspection in the Tampa Bay area in 2026 runs approximately $350 to $550 for a typical 1,800 to 2,500 square foot Valrico home. Larger homes above 3,000 square feet typically run $500 to $700. Expect to pay more for a thorough inspector who takes three to four hours rather than two. A cheap, fast inspection is often not worth what you pay for it.

Florida home inspectors must be licensed by the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR). You can verify any inspector's license at [myfloridalicense.com](https://www.myfloridalicense.com/wl11.asp). Do not use an inspector who cannot provide a Florida DBPR license number.

## The Top Issues Found in East Hillsborough County Homes

### Roofs: The Single Highest-Cost Item

Roof condition is the most consequential inspection finding in Valrico, and it has two separate implications: structural and insurance. Florida insurance carriers have tightened underwriting standards significantly over the last five years. Most major carriers will not write a new policy on a roof that is more than 15 to 20 years old, depending on material. Some are stricter.

What to watch for in a Valrico home inspection: granule loss on asphalt shingles, damaged or missing flashing around chimneys and skylights, soft spots in the decking visible from the attic, and signs of active or historical leaking including water stains on attic trusses or ceiling joists.

Roof replacement costs in Hillsborough County in 2026 range from approximately $15,000 to $35,000 depending on roof size, material, and pitch. A roof credit at closing of this magnitude is legitimate in a buyer's market, and sellers with aging roofs are routinely granting it.

### HVAC Systems: Heat and Humidity Accelerate Wear

An air conditioning system in Valrico runs 10 to 11 months per year. That relentless operating cycle means systems wear faster than the national average suggests. The expected useful life of an HVAC system in central Florida is approximately 12 to 15 years for a typical residential split system. A system installed in 2010 is approaching end of service life. A 2007 system that hasn't been replaced is on borrowed time.

What to watch for: age of the air handler and compressor (required by law to be posted on the unit), evidence of refrigerant leaks at the coil, rust on the air handler cabinet or drain pan, and the condition of the ductwork in the attic. Attic duct runs in Florida are exposed to extreme heat and humidity and can deteriorate or develop mold if the system is oversized or if there are return air pathway issues.

HVAC replacement costs in Hillsborough County in 2026: approximately $6,000 to $12,000 for a standard 3 to 5 ton split system, depending on capacity and efficiency rating. A system in poor condition is a negotiating point. The current Valrico buyer's market -- with 240 to 260 active listings as of November 2026 -- means sellers are frequently accepting HVAC credits at or near replacement cost. See the [November 2026 Valrico market report](/blog/valrico-fl-real-estate-market-update-november-2026) for current leverage conditions.

### Plumbing: Polybutylene Pipes and Aging Infrastructure

Polybutylene pipe was installed in Florida homes from the late 1970s through approximately 1995. The material, which is a gray or blue flexible plastic, was discontinued after widespread failure reports involving chlorine-induced degradation. Homes in Bloomingdale's oldest sections and in some early Buckhorn properties may still have polybutylene supply lines if they have not been repiped.

Identifying polybutylene: the pipes are gray, silver, or blue and have plastic or metal push-fit fittings. If your inspector identifies polybutylene, obtain a licensed plumber's estimate for a full repipe. In a typical Valrico home, a full PEX repipe runs approximately $4,000 to $8,000 depending on home size and pipe accessibility.

Other plumbing items commonly flagged in east Hillsborough County homes: sediment buildup in older water heaters (10-plus years), undersized return lines on second-story bathrooms, and slow drain conditions that indicate partial blockages or root intrusion in sewer laterals. For homes with well and septic systems -- less common in Valrico's established neighborhoods but present in some larger-lot properties -- separate well water testing and septic inspection are essential.

### Electrical: Panels, GFCI, and Code Items

Electrical issues in Valrico homes typically fall into three categories: aging panel boxes, missing GFCI protection, and open permits or unpermitted work.

Federal Pacific Electric (FPE) Stab-Lok panels were installed widely in the 1960s through 1980s and have a documented failure-to-trip rate that makes them a fire risk. These panels appear occasionally in the oldest Valrico homes. If an inspector identifies an FPE panel, budget for replacement ($1,500 to $2,500 for a standard panel swap) and expect most insurance carriers to require it.

GFCI (ground fault circuit interrupter) protection is required by current code near water sources including kitchen counters, bathrooms, garages, and exterior outlets. Many Valrico homes from the 1990s were built to the code of that era and may have partial GFCI coverage. A licensed electrician can bring the home up to current standards for $300 to $900 depending on the number of outlets involved.

Unpermitted additions -- screened enclosures, garages converted to living space, secondary bathroom additions -- are present in a portion of the Valrico resale inventory. Unpermitted work does not automatically disqualify a purchase, but it adds liability. The buyer assumes responsibility for the work after closing and may be required to bring it into compliance at their expense if Hillsborough County inspects for a future permit or if an insurance claim involves the unpermitted area.

### Moisture, Mold, and Building Envelope

Florida's humidity creates a constant moisture load on every building surface. What manifests as a stain on a bedroom ceiling may be a minor historic leak long since resolved, or it may be active moisture intrusion from a compromised roof, window, or exterior wall penetration. Inspectors can identify evidence of moisture with surface moisture meters and by visual examination, but they cannot see inside walls.

Stucco homes, which represent a significant portion of Valrico's housing stock, require attention to hairline cracking around windows and penetrations. Properly sealed stucco cracks are cosmetic. Cracking that has allowed water entry behind the stucco layer can cause wood rot and mold at the framing level. Remediation when it reaches the framing level is a substantial expense.

If your inspector identifies suspicious moisture readings or visible mold, consider a separate mold assessment by a licensed indoor environmental professional. This runs $300 to $600 for a standard residential sampling and can identify whether visible growth is cosmetic (surface mold on drywall from a resolved moisture event) or indicative of systemic intrusion.

## Specialty Inspections Every Valrico Buyer Should Consider

### WDO (Wood-Destroying Organism) Inspection

A WDO inspection -- commonly called a termite inspection -- is a separate service performed by a licensed pest control company. It is not part of the standard home inspection and must be ordered and paid for separately. Cost runs approximately $75 to $150 for a typical Valrico home.

Florida is subterranean termite territory. Formosan termites and Eastern subterranean termites are present throughout Hillsborough County and cause substantial structural damage before they are detected by homeowners. A WDO inspection covers termites, drywood termites, wood-boring beetles, and fungi that cause structural wood deterioration. The inspector's report will identify active infestations, previous treatments, and areas of wood damage. Some lenders require a clean WDO report as a condition of financing.

For more context on buyer costs including inspection fees, see the full [Valrico buyer closing costs breakdown](/blog/buyer-closing-costs-valrico-fl-2026).

### Wind Mitigation Inspection

A wind mitigation inspection evaluates the structural features that reduce hurricane damage risk: roof shape, roof deck attachment method, roof covering type, roof-to-wall connections, and opening protection (windows and doors). The report is submitted to your insurance carrier and can produce meaningful annual premium discounts.

Cost: approximately $75 to $150. Potential annual insurance savings: $400 to $1,500 depending on what the home's construction qualifies for. For a home with a hip roof (four-sided roof) and hurricane straps connecting the roof truss to the wall framing, the premium discount is often large enough to pay for the inspection many times over.

### Four-Point Inspection

Many Florida insurance carriers require a four-point inspection for homes older than 30 years before they will issue a new homeowners policy. The four-point covers the four main systems that drive insurance claims: roof, HVAC, electrical, and plumbing. It is typically required at the insurer's request and can be performed by the same licensed home inspector who does the general inspection, often for an additional $75 to $150. Ask your inspector to include it at the time of the standard inspection to avoid a second trip fee.

## Using Inspection Results in Valrico's Current Buyer's Market

As of November 2026, Valrico is unambiguously in buyer's market territory. In this environment, inspection results are negotiating tools, not deal-killers. The common approaches:

**Repair credit at closing:** The seller provides a dollar credit against closing costs or purchase price in lieu of making repairs. Credits of $5,000 to $15,000 are common on homes with aging roofs or HVAC systems. Buyers often prefer credits because they can control the contractor selection and timing.

**Seller completes specific repairs:** More common for items that lenders require to be fixed before funding, such as a wood-destroying organism treatment or an active roof leak. Sellers typically use their own contractors, so buyers should verify completion with a re-inspection or final walkthrough.

**Price reduction:** Less common for inspection issues but applicable when the scope of deferred maintenance is broad and the market data supports a lower value.

One thing buyers should know: waiving the inspection contingency to compete in Valrico in 2026 is not a sound strategy. The market no longer demands it. In a buyer's market with 240-plus listings and homes averaging 50-plus days before going under contract, the sellers who are not willing to allow an inspection are sellers with something to hide. Inspect every property.
