## Bloomingdale (33594): Upper Range, Larger Lots

The Bloomingdale area in 33594 runs from Bloomingdale Avenue south toward the 33596 border. At $450,000, you are at the top of the Bloomingdale market, which means more negotiating power and more selection than you would have had six months ago.

**What $450,000 buys in Bloomingdale:**
- 2,200 to 2,800 square feet
- 4 bedrooms, 2 to 3 bathrooms
- Homes built primarily in the 1990s to early 2000s
- Lot sizes of 0.18 to 0.30 acres, often with mature oaks and established landscaping
- Some homes with private pools (common in the 1990s build era for this area)
- Price per square foot: approximately $160 to $200

**School zone:** Bloomingdale High School zone (B-rated by state, strong athletic programs). Middle school feeders include Barrington and McLane. This is not the Newsome zone, which matters for resale to school-focused families.

**Current market reality:** Bloomingdale listings at $450,000 have typically been on the market 55 to 75 days. At that point sellers are motivated. Roof age is the key inspection risk in this vintage: homes built in the mid-1990s with original roofs are overdue for replacement, and insurance carriers are increasingly refusing to write coverage on roofs over 15 years. Budget for a $15,000 to $22,000 roof replacement if the inspection reveals deferred maintenance on this point.

**Best fit for:** Buyers who want a larger lot, mature neighborhood character, and maximum square footage per dollar. Families who are comfortable with Bloomingdale HS zone or who have younger children not yet at the high school selection stage.

At $450,000, you are likely getting a home in the Newsome zone, but verify the specific street address before making an offer. The Hillsborough County Schools boundary locator is authoritative; do not rely on listing agent statements alone.

**Current market reality:** Buckhorn at $450,000 moves a bit faster than Bloomingdale at the same price, specifically because Newsome zone buyers are more consistent in their demand. Expect 35 to 55 days on market for well-priced Buckhorn homes. If a Buckhorn home in the Newsome zone is sitting at 60-plus days, there is usually a reason: overpricing, deferred maintenance, or a condition issue. Investigate before negotiating.

**Best fit for:** Buyers who want Newsome zone access at an accessible price point and are willing to accept smaller square footage and lot size compared to what $450,000 buys in Bloomingdale.

Inspection diligence is critical on 1990s-era River Hills homes: electrical panels, roof, HVAC, and pool equipment all need scrutiny.

**Best fit for:** Buyers who prioritize gated community living and Newsome zone access over square footage and are prepared for 1990s construction with potential deferred maintenance.

## Neighborhoods to Avoid at $450,000

A word of caution: the $450,000 budget creates pressure to find a way into 33596 and the Newsome zone. Some buyers overstretch by purchasing a home with serious deferred maintenance at $445,000 to $450,000 when the realistic cost to bring it up to current standards adds another $40,000 to $80,000. At that point, you have effectively paid $490,000 to $530,000 for a home that may not comp at that value.

Barrett's standard advice: get the full inspection including roof certification, four-point (required by most FL carriers for older homes), and wind mitigation. If the inspection comes back with a $30,000-plus repair list on a home at $450,000, the right answer is often to buy a better-conditioned home in 33594 at $420,000 and use the difference to build equity without immediate repair exposure.
