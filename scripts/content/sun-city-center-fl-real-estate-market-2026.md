Sun City Center, Florida is one of the largest and most established active adult retirement communities in the United States. Located in southern Hillsborough County about 25 miles southeast of Tampa and 20 miles east of Sarasota, it draws retirees from across the country who want year-round warm weather, an active lifestyle, and a community built entirely around 55-plus living. If you are considering retiring to the Tampa Bay area or helping a parent find a retirement home in Hillsborough County, Sun City Center deserves a close look. Here is what the current market actually shows in 2026, and how this community compares to other retirement options in the region.

## Sun City Center at a Glance: 2026 Market Data

Sun City Center (ZIP code 33573) is a buyer's market in 2026, driven by a combination of elevated inventory, affordability pressure on older condo stock, and the broader Hillsborough County repricing that has affected every submarket this year.

**Key mid-2026 metrics:**

- **Median sale price:** $262,500 to $305,000 depending on the trailing period, with the 12-month trailing median near $305,000 and the most recent months (May to July 2026) landing closer to $262,500 to $285,000
- **Year-over-year change:** Down approximately 9.5% from the same period in 2025, one of the steeper declines in south Hillsborough
- **Days on market:** 58 to 68 days, somewhat faster than the broader Hillsborough County average and meaningfully faster than Apollo Beach (95 to 105 days) for comparable price ranges
- **Sale-to-list ratio:** Approximately 97.4%, meaning sellers are typically closing within 2.5% of their asking price
- **Months of supply:** Approximately 1.63 months, which is technically in seller territory but at a price point and buyer pool that creates a different dynamic than typical suburban markets

The 9.5% year-over-year price decline is the most important number for buyers and sellers to understand. It is not a random drop. It reflects two converging forces: the general Hillsborough County market repricing that has brought prices down 3.5 to 10% depending on the submarket, and the specific affordability and insurance stress that is hitting older condo and villa stock harder than single-family homes in younger communities.

## What $200K to $450K Buys in Sun City Center in 2026

The price range in Sun City Center is dramatically wider than most buyers expect. The difference between a Kings Point villa and a newer single-family home in Renaissance can exceed $200,000. Here is what the tiers look like in practice.

**Under $250,000:** This tier is almost entirely older condo and villa stock in the original Sun City Center sections and Kings Point. You will find attached villas with 1,200 to 1,500 square feet, one to two bedrooms, one to two bathrooms, and carport parking rather than a garage. These units were built in the 1970s through 1990s and carry deferred maintenance exposure. HOA fees in this tier typically run $400 to $700 per month and cover exterior maintenance, water, cable, and community amenities. The monthly fee partially offsets the low purchase price.

**$250,000 to $350,000:** This is the core of the Sun City Center market. In this range, buyers can access larger villas and smaller single-family homes in Kings Point, well-maintained attached units in newer sections of the original community, and entry-level single-family homes in the outer sections of Sun City Center near SR-674. At this price point, you start to find two-car garages, screened lanais, and updated interiors.

**$350,000 to $500,000:** Renaissance and the newer single-family sections of Kings Point fall in this tier. These homes are typically 1,600 to 2,200 square feet, built in the 2000s to 2010s, with two to three bedrooms and two bathrooms. Renaissance in particular offers more contemporary finishes and access to the Renaissance Golf Club and country club restaurant. HOA fees in Renaissance run $200 to $350 per month.

**Above $500,000:** The Sun City Center market thin out above $500,000. You will find some larger water-view homes and renovated premium properties, but buyers at this price point are more likely to cross-shop with Apollo Beach waterfront or the newer 55-plus sections of Wimauma such as Medley at SouthShore Bay or Valencia Lakes.

## The Three Main Communities: Original SCC, Kings Point, and Renaissance

Sun City Center is not a single neighborhood. It is a collection of communities that share the same retirement-focused character but differ significantly in price, age, HOA structure, and lifestyle.

### Sun City Center Original

The original Sun City Center community was developed by Del Webb beginning in 1961. It covers a large section of the 33573 ZIP code and includes thousands of single-family homes and attached villas that were built across several decades. The original section is served by the Sun City Center Community Association (SCCCA), which operates a massive amenity complex including multiple pools, fitness centers, sports courts, craft studios, and an 18-hole public golf course. The SCCCA annual fee was historically around $330 per year, a remarkably low amount for what it includes, though residents also pay for specific amenities they use.

Homes in the original SCC are typically older, with most built between the 1960s and 1990s. Buyers get more square footage per dollar than in Kings Point or Renaissance, but they take on more renovation risk. Buyers who are comfortable with renovation projects and can budget for a kitchen or bathroom update often find exceptional value here.

### Kings Point

Kings Point is the most amenity-dense community in Sun City Center and arguably one of the most amenity-rich 55-plus communities in Florida. The gated community includes approximately 5,277 attached villas and single-family homes housing around 9,500 residents. The centerpiece is the 90,000-square-foot Kings Point Clubhouse, which includes indoor and outdoor pools, a fitness center, the Borini Theater for live performances, hobby rooms, billiards, and a full cafe. A second South Clubhouse adds a resort-style pool and additional activity space. Kings Point has 27 holes of golf, six pools, and hundreds of active social clubs and classes.

HOA fees in Kings Point vary by section and sub-association. Budget a range of $400 to $600 per month for the typical Kings Point villa, with some sections running higher. These fees cover exterior maintenance, cable, internet, water, and access to all Kings Point amenities. For buyers coming from Northeast or Midwest climates where maintaining a home through winter is a burden, the all-inclusive fee structure of Kings Point is often the deciding factor.

Kings Point prices in mid-2026 run from roughly $175,000 for a small one-bedroom villa to $400,000 for a larger updated single-family home on a premium lot. The median is closer to $225,000 to $265,000, making Kings Point the most affordable option on a purchase-price basis in the Sun City Center area. Monthly costs increase when HOA fees are factored in.

### Renaissance

Renaissance is the newest large section of Sun City Center, developed primarily in the 2000s and 2010s. It is a gated community with a different character from Kings Point. Renaissance centers on a private country club with a restaurant, social events, and the Renaissance Golf Club, an 18-hole championship course. Homes in Renaissance are more contemporary in design, typically built with concrete block construction, tile roofs, and modern floor plans.

Single-family homes in Renaissance run from approximately $340,000 to $500,000 in mid-2026. The community targets active retirees who want a country club lifestyle without the heavy maintenance burden of a larger suburban home. HOA fees in Renaissance run $200 to $350 per month and cover grounds maintenance and club access.

## Golf: A Core Part of the Sun City Center Value Proposition

For golf-focused retirees, Sun City Center is among the best destinations in Florida. The greater Sun City Center area has eight golf courses with 162 total holes. Both championship and executive courses are available, and the community is home to the Ben Sutton Golf School, which offers instruction for golfers at every level. Buyers who are comparing Sun City Center to other retirement destinations on a golf basis will find that the density and variety of golf options here is difficult to match at the price point.

## Insurance and Carrying Costs: The Real Monthly Number

One of the most common buyer surprises in Sun City Center is the gap between the purchase price and the actual monthly cost of ownership. A $265,000 Kings Point villa sounds affordable, but the full monthly picture looks different:

- **Mortgage:** At 6.25% on $265,000 with 20% down, principal and interest runs approximately $1,306 per month
- **Property taxes:** At a $265,000 assessed value with homestead exemption, estimate $3,500 to $4,200 per year ($290 to $350 per month)
- **Homeowner's insurance:** For a condo or villa in Sun City Center, budget $1,800 to $3,500 per year ($150 to $290 per month). Flood zone varies by specific property, so verify the FEMA designation before buying
- **HOA fees:** Kings Point runs $400 to $600 per month; original SCC runs $50 to $150 per month plus SCCCA fees; Renaissance runs $200 to $350 per month

The total monthly cost for a median Kings Point villa can easily run $2,200 to $2,600 per month when all carrying costs are included. That is the number buyers should underwrite against their retirement income, not just the mortgage payment.

## Sun City Center vs. Other 55-Plus Options in South Hillsborough

Sun City Center is not the only 55-plus community option in southern Hillsborough County. Buyers comparing the market should consider the other major retirement-focused communities in the area.

**Vs. Valencia Lakes (Wimauma):** Valencia Lakes is a strictly 55-plus community in nearby Wimauma (33598). It offers a larger amenity package than the original SCC sections, with a 28,000-square-foot clubhouse, resort-style pool, and restaurant. Prices in Valencia Lakes are typically $350,000 to $500,000, higher than most of Sun City Center. Valencia Lakes does not have the golf density that Sun City Center offers.

**Vs. Medley at SouthShore Bay (Wimauma):** Medley is a newer 55-plus section within the SouthShore Bay master-planned community, which includes a 5.5-acre Crystal Lagoon. It offers a distinctly different lifestyle with a younger buyer profile and more resort-like amenities. Prices start around $350,000 and go considerably higher. It is newer construction, which reduces maintenance risk.

**Vs. Apollo Beach:** Apollo Beach (33572) is not a 55-plus community, but retirees frequently cross-shop it for waterfront access. Median prices in Apollo Beach are $479,000 to $538,000, significantly higher than Sun City Center, and the insurance cost for waterfront properties can exceed $10,000 per year for combined homeowners and flood coverage.

**Sun City Center wins on:** Price, golf access, the scale of social infrastructure, and the fact that it is a proven, fully established retirement destination rather than a newer community still building out its amenities.

## Is Sun City Center a Good Investment in 2026?

Sun City Center is not a traditional investment market. The 55-plus restriction means resale is limited to age-qualified buyers, which is a smaller pool than the general market. The 9.5% year-over-year price decline should not be ignored by buyers who see this as a short-term hold.

That said, buyers who are purchasing for lifestyle and intend to hold 10 or more years have a reasonable case. The Tampa Bay area's retirement demographic continues to grow. I-75 access from Sun City Center is excellent, and the community is 20 to 25 minutes from Sarasota-Bradenton International Airport and 35 minutes from Tampa International. As the baby boomer retirement wave continues, demand for established 55-plus communities with proven infrastructure should remain steady.

For buyers specifically considering a second home or snowbird property in Sun City Center, the condo and villa market at $175,000 to $265,000 offers the best acquisition math. Rentals in age-restricted communities face occupancy restrictions, so verify what is and is not permitted before purchasing with rental income in mind.

## What Buyers Should Know Before Making an Offer in Sun City Center

1. **Verify the HOA rules and financials before closing.** Sun City Center has multiple HOA layers depending on the section (SCCCA, Kings Point sub-association, Renaissance HOA). Request at least 24 months of HOA financials and meeting minutes before committing. Underfunded reserves are a material risk in communities with older common infrastructure.

2. **Order a thorough inspection.** Homes built in the 1960s through 1990s may carry original roofing, plumbing, and electrical systems. A four-point inspection is required for most Florida insurance policies and will flag issues before closing.

3. **Get insurance quotes before making an offer.** Florida's insurance market is volatile, and quotes can vary significantly based on roof age, construction type, and flood zone. A $265,000 villa with a 2005 roof and no flood zone designation will carry dramatically different insurance costs than a $265,000 villa with a 1985 roof in a low-lying area.

4. **Understand the activity fee structure.** In the original SCC sections, some amenities require separate use fees or membership. In Kings Point, the bundled HOA covers most amenities. Ask specifically which facilities are included in the monthly fee and which require additional payment.

## The Bottom Line on Sun City Center FL in 2026

Sun City Center is a genuine value for retirees who want a full-scale active adult lifestyle in Hillsborough County at a price point that most comparable markets cannot match. The combination of golf density, social infrastructure, affordable entry points in the $175,000 to $350,000 range, and a proven 60-year track record makes it one of the most complete retirement communities in Florida.

The 9.5% year-over-year price decline and the current buyer leverage make 2026 a reasonable entry point for well-qualified buyers who are making a long-term lifestyle decision. The risk, as always in older community stock, is the carrying cost: HOA fees, insurance, and potential special assessments for aging common infrastructure. Buyers who budget for these costs realistically and find the right section for their lifestyle will find Sun City Center delivers on its promise.
