## The Four Approaches to Buying and Selling at the Same Time

### Approach 1: Sell First, Then Buy (Lowest Risk)

The lowest-risk approach. You list your Valrico home, accept an offer, and use the net proceeds to fund your next purchase. No bridge financing required.

**The challenge:** You need somewhere to live between closing on your sale and closing on your purchase.

**Three ways Valrico sellers handle this:**

**1. Negotiate a leaseback (seller occupancy agreement):** You sell your home and immediately execute a Seller Occupancy Agreement where you stay in the property after closing as a tenant -- typically for 30 to 90 days at an agreed daily rate. The buyer receives possession at the end of the leaseback period. In the current Valrico market, 60-day leasebacks are negotiated regularly. The daily rate is typically set at the buyer's new daily mortgage cost (principal and interest, divided by 30), which is usually $80 to $130 per day on a typical Valrico purchase.

**2. Extend the closing timeline:** If the buyer of your home agrees, push the closing date to 60 or 75 days (versus a typical 30 to 45 days) to give yourself time to find and close on the next home. In the current market, buyers are often flexible on closing timelines because their own underwriting and inspection process can take 50 to 60 days anyway.

**3. Short-term housing:** Airbnb, an extended-stay hotel, or temporarily moving in with family while you search for and close on the next home. This works best if you have a clear target area and can move quickly once you find the right property.

Barrett: "In this market, I'm routinely getting sellers a 60-day leaseback. The buyer gets the house; the seller gets 60 days to find the next home and close. It is cleaner than a bridge loan and costs nothing beyond the daily rental rate."

### Approach 2: Contingent Offer (Most Common in Current Valrico Market)

A contingent offer means your purchase of the new home is conditional on selling your existing Valrico home. If your current home does not go under contract within a specified timeframe (typically 30 to 60 days), the purchase contract terminates and your earnest money is returned.

**When this works in November 2026 Valrico:**
- You are targeting sellers who have been on market 45 or more days (significantly more receptive to contingencies)
- Your current Valrico home is priced accurately and genuinely likely to sell within the contingency window
- You are prepared to offer a slightly above-ask price or other favorable terms to offset the seller's risk in accepting a contingency

**The standard kick-out clause:** When a seller accepts a contingent offer, they typically retain the right to continue marketing the home and accept a competing non-contingent offer. If they receive another offer, they issue a "kick-out" notice, giving you typically 48 to 72 hours to either remove the sale contingency or release the contract.

**What this means in practice:** In November 2026, the kick-out clause is less threatening than it sounds. With 50 to 68 average DOM, sellers in the $400,000 to $530,000 range are not receiving back-to-back offers. If your current Valrico home is priced at current market value and actively listed, most sellers will wait 30 to 45 days.

**Key success factor for contingent offers in Valrico:** Have a Comparative Market Analysis (CMA) on your current home ready to share with the seller. When a listing agent can show their client that the buyer's home is priced at current comps and located in a neighborhood with a 25 to 45 day DOM, the contingency feels manageable rather than speculative.

### Approach 3: Bridge Loan (Most Flexible, Highest Cost)

A bridge loan is a short-term loan secured by your current home's equity that provides cash to close on your new home before your old home sells. You then repay the bridge loan when your existing home closes.

**How bridge loans work:**
- Lender loans against your current home's equity, typically up to 70 to 80 percent combined loan-to-value
- Term: typically 6 to 12 months
- Interest rate: typically prime plus 1 to 2 percent (with current prime approximately 8.5 percent, bridge rates typically run 9.5 to 10.5 percent)
- Origination fee: typically 1 to 1.5 percent of the bridge loan amount
- Repaid in full when your existing home closes

**Example for a Valrico move-up buyer:**

Homeowner in 33594 with:
- Current home value: $390,000
- Existing mortgage balance: $175,000
- Available equity: $215,000
- Target new home: $530,000 in 33596 (Newsome zone)
- 20 percent down payment needed: $106,000

Bridge loan structure:
- Lend 70 percent of current home value: $273,000
- Less existing mortgage: $175,000
- Available from bridge: $98,000
- Out-of-pocket to close gap to $106,000 target: $8,000

Bridge loan carrying cost (assuming 4-month bridge period):
- $98,000 at 10 percent for 4 months: $3,267 in interest
- Origination fee (1.25 percent): $1,225
- Total bridge cost: approximately $4,500

That $4,500 is real money, but for a buyer who finds the right Newsome zone home and needs to move without a contingency, the bridge cost is often worth the certainty.

**When bridge loans make sense:**
- You have identified a specific home that may not stay available through a 30 to 60 day contingency window
- You have sufficient equity to support the bridge loan without pushing your combined debt-to-income ratio past the lender's limit
- You are willing to carry two sets of housing costs for 60 to 120 days

**Bridge loan cautions in 2026:** You will be qualifying for two mortgages during the bridge period (your existing loan plus the new purchase loan). Your debt-to-income ratio must accommodate both payments plus the bridge loan interest, which can be a challenge. Work with a mortgage broker to run the numbers before assuming you qualify.

Not all lenders offer bridge loans. Local community banks and credit unions in Hillsborough County are often the best source, as the major national lenders have largely moved away from residential bridge products.

### Approach 4: Access Existing Equity via HELOC

A Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC) is a revolving line of credit secured by your current home's equity. Rather than a bridge loan, some Valrico homeowners use a HELOC to fund the down payment on the new purchase before their old home closes.

**Current HELOC terms (Hillsborough County, November 2026):**
- Typically up to 85 to 90 percent combined loan-to-value
- Variable rate: typically prime plus 0 to 1 percent (approximately 8.5 to 9.5 percent)
- More flexible draw schedule than a bridge loan
- Smaller origination fees than bridge loans

**The critical catch in 2026:** If your existing mortgage is at 3.5 to 4.5 percent (the common range for Valrico homes purchased in 2020 to 2022), a HELOC adds variable-rate debt on top of your existing low-rate first mortgage. Many homeowners in this situation find the HELOC preferable to a cash-out refinance, which would require replacing their low-rate first mortgage with a new loan at current 7.10 percent rates.

A HELOC for $80,000 at 9.5 percent adds approximately $633 per month in interest to your carrying costs during the transition period. That is meaningful but typically less expensive than losing the right home.

## Practical Timeline: What 90 Days Looks Like

Here is a realistic 90-day scenario for a Valrico simultaneous transaction starting in late November 2026:

**Month 1 (November-December):**
- Get pre-approved for the new purchase, factoring in your current mortgage balance and existing monthly payment
- List your current home at accurate market value based on current 33594 or 33596 comps
- Begin active showings
- Identify target neighborhoods and price range for the new home
- Tour homes; write an offer if you find the right one (contingent if needed)

**Month 2 (December-January):**
- Accept offer on your current home; negotiate a 45 to 60 day leaseback
- If you wrote a contingent offer on the new home, remove the sale contingency now that you are under contract on your current sale
- Order inspection on new home; negotiate any repair credits or seller concessions
- Process mortgage underwriting on new purchase

**Month 3 (January-February):**
- Close on new home purchase using net sale proceeds as down payment
- Leaseback period on old home ends; move into new home
- Final close on sale of old home (if not simultaneous)

**Key coordination point:** The ideal scenario closes both transactions within one to five days of each other. Many Hillsborough County title companies facilitate same-day or consecutive-day closings for buyers and sellers coordinating this scenario. To receive the homestead exemption on your new Valrico home for the 2027 tax year, you must own and occupy the property as your primary residence on January 1, 2027. Close before December 31 and you can file for the exemption. Close January 2 and you wait until 2028.

For a home assessed at $500,000 in 33596, the standard homestead exemption saves approximately $750 per year in property taxes in Hillsborough County. ## Preparing Your Valrico Home for a Simultaneous Transaction

The move-up buyer who wants to execute a smooth simultaneous transaction has two properties to think about: the one they are selling and the one they are buying.

**On the sale side:**
- Price your Valrico home based on November and October 2026 comparable sales, not spring peak pricing
- Complete minor repairs and paint touch-ups before listing; FHA buyers (a significant pool in the $350,000 to $420,000 range) need homes to pass FHA appraisal
- Disclose everything accurately; delays caused by undisclosed issues discovered during inspection kill timelines
- In 33594, budget for 25 to 50 days of active marketing before you are under contract at a correctly priced listing

**On the purchase side:**
- Get pre-approved before you submit an offer; a simultaneous transaction with no pre-approval is not credible to sellers
- Budget for seller concessions in the current market -- closing cost credits, rate buydowns, and repair credits are common in the $400,000 to $550,000 range
- Target homes that have been on market 45 days or more; they are more likely to accept a contingent offer or negotiate aggressively on price

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I buy a house before selling my current home in Valrico FL?**
Yes, but it requires planning. The most common approaches are a bridge loan (borrow against your current home's equity for the down payment), a contingent offer (buy the new home contingent on selling your existing one), or accessing equity via HELOC. Barrett works with Valrico clients on all three approaches depending on their timeline, equity position, and lender qualification situation.

**How long does a contingency period last in a Valrico real estate contract?**
A contingency period is negotiated, but typically runs 30 to 60 days in a Valrico purchase contract. The seller retains a kick-out clause allowing them to accept a competing non-contingent offer, which gives you typically 48 to 72 hours to either remove your contingency or release the contract.

**What is a leaseback agreement in real estate?**
A leaseback (also called a rent-back or seller occupancy agreement) lets the seller of a home stay in the property after closing as a tenant, paying a daily rental rate, for a negotiated period -- typically 30 to 90 days. The buyer receives possession at the end of the leaseback period. In the current Valrico market, 30 to 60 day leasebacks are negotiated regularly.

**What does a bridge loan cost in Hillsborough County?**
Bridge loan rates in the current market typically run prime plus 1 to 2 percent (approximately 9.5 to 10.5 percent). On a $100,000 bridge used for 4 months, expect approximately $4,000 to $5,500 in total interest and origination costs. Bridge loans are available through local community banks and credit unions; most major national lenders no longer offer residential bridge products.

**How do I know if my current Valrico home will sell quickly enough to support a simultaneous transaction?**
Request a Comparative Market Analysis from Barrett before listing. A correctly priced home in 33594 is currently moving in 25 to 50 days; a correctly priced home in the Newsome zone in 33596 is moving in 30 to 55 days. If your home is priced at or slightly below current comparable sales, a 45 to 60 day contingency window is achievable.

**Is it better to buy or sell first in Valrico FL right now?**
For most Valrico move-up buyers in late 2026, the right answer is to list your current home and begin seriously shopping simultaneously. If you find the right target home and need to write a contingent offer, do it. If the seller won't accept a contingency, pivot to a bridge loan conversation. The majority of transactions in the current Valrico market can be structured without a bridge loan if you are realistic about timeline and willing to negotiate creatively.
