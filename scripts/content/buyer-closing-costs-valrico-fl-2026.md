## Hillsborough County Title Insurance: Why It's Different Here

This is the biggest distinction between Hillsborough County and many other parts of Florida and the country. In most Florida counties, the seller traditionally selects the title company and pays for the owner's title insurance policy. **In Hillsborough County, the buyer typically selects the title company and pays for both the owner's and lender's title insurance policies.**

This is not a law, it is a local custom. It is spelled out in the standard FAR/BAR contract, which designates the county of the property as determining who selects the title agent and pays for title insurance. For Hillsborough County, the buyer pays.

**What title insurance actually costs on a $415,000 purchase:**
- Owner's policy: approximately $1,750 to $2,100 (based on a promulgated rate that is set by Florida statute and applies uniformly to all Florida title companies)
- Lender's policy (simultaneous issue): approximately $275 to $400
- Combined: approximately $2,025 to $2,500

The promulgated rate means you cannot shop for a lower price on the insurance itself, but you can shop for lower settlement and closing agent fees across different title companies. The difference between the cheapest and most expensive title companies in the Tampa Bay area can be $300 to $600 on the ancillary fees.

## Florida Intangible Tax and Documentary Stamps

Two Florida-specific taxes affect Valrico home purchases:

### Documentary Stamp Tax on the Deed
**Rate:** $0.70 per $100 of purchase price (or any portion thereof)
**Who pays:** In Hillsborough County custom, the **seller** traditionally pays the doc stamps on the deed
**Amount on $415,000 purchase:** $2,905

Doc stamps on the deed are almost always a seller cost in Hillsborough County. However, the FAR/BAR contract makes this negotiable. In a buyer's market like 2026, sellers accepting closing cost contributions often structure them as a lump-sum credit at closing, so the technical "who pays" matters less than your net cost after negotiations.

### Florida Intangible Tax on the Mortgage
**Rate:** $0.002 per $1 of mortgage amount (0.20%)
**Who pays:** The **buyer** always pays this tax
**Amount on a $332,000 mortgage:** $664

This is not negotiable and applies to all mortgages in Florida. Cash buyers do not pay it.

### Documentary Stamp Tax on the Mortgage Note
**Rate:** $0.35 per $100 of mortgage amount
**Who pays:** The **buyer**
**Amount on $332,000 mortgage:** $1,162

**Combined Florida mortgage taxes (buyer):** Approximately $1,826 on a $332,000 mortgage.

## Lender Fees: Where the Range Is Widest

Lender fees vary more than almost any other closing cost category. For a conventional 30-year fixed loan in 2026 at approximately 6.0% to 6.5%, here is what buyers typically see:

- **Origination fee or discount points:** Ranges from $0 (no-cost loan with slightly higher rate) to 1.0% or more of the loan amount for a rate buydown. On a $332,000 loan, 1 point = $3,320. Evaluate whether the rate reduction is worth the upfront cost based on your expected holding period.
- **Processing fee:** $400 to $800. This covers the lender's administrative work.
- **Underwriting fee:** $400 to $900. Covers risk assessment.
- **Flood certification fee:** $10 to $25. Required on all loans.
- **Tax service fee:** $50 to $80.

**How to compare lenders correctly:** Use the Loan Estimate (required within three business days of application) to compare fees. Look at the "Origination Charges" section of Section A and "Services You Cannot Shop For" in Section B. These are the costs that vary between lenders. Section C items (title, settlement) you can shop yourself.

In 2026, some lenders are offering closing cost credits in lieu of lower rates. Run the math carefully. A $5,000 lender credit that raises your rate from 6.25% to 6.625% on a $332,000 loan costs you an extra $95/month in principal and interest. That means the credit pays for itself in about 53 months. If you plan to stay longer than 4.5 years, the lower rate wins.

## Prepaid Items: Not Fees, But Real Cash Required

Three prepaid items require cash at closing that many buyers do not anticipate:

### First Year Homeowner's Insurance
You must pay the first full year of your homeowner's insurance premium at closing (most lenders require it). In Valrico in 2026, insurance costs for a standard 3-4 bedroom single-family home run:

- Homes with roofs under 10 years old: $2,800 to $3,800/year
- Homes with roofs 10 to 15 years old: $3,200 to $4,500/year
- Homes with roofs over 15 years old: $4,500 to $6,500+/year

If you are purchasing a home with an older roof, the insurance premium cost can add significantly to your closing-day cash requirement.

### Prepaid Mortgage Interest
You will pay interest from your closing date through the end of that month. If you close on the 15th of November, you pay 15 days of interest. On a $332,000 loan at 6.25%, that is approximately $57/day. A mid-month close costs about $855 in prepaid interest. Closing at the end of the month minimizes this cost.

### Property Tax Escrow
Your lender will typically collect two to three months of property taxes into escrow at closing. On a $415,000 Valrico home, annual property taxes with homestead exemption run approximately $5,800 to $6,800. Two months equals approximately $967 to $1,133. This money is held in escrow and used to pay your tax bill when it comes due, so it is not "lost," but it is cash out of pocket at closing.

## HOA-Related Closing Costs

If you are purchasing in a Valrico neighborhood with an HOA, expect some or all of these additional charges:

- **HOA transfer fee:** $100 to $500. Charged by the HOA to process the ownership change.
- **HOA capital contribution fee:** $200 to $2,000. Some HOAs require a one-time contribution to the reserve fund when a new buyer takes ownership. This is disclosed in the HOA documents and is not negotiable.
- **HOA estoppel letter fee:** $150 to $400. Required by lenders to verify HOA dues, any unpaid assessments, and pending violations. This is typically a seller cost but verify in your contract.
- **HOA document review:** Buyers in Florida have three business days to review HOA documents after receipt. Some sellers pay for the document package; others do not. Budget $150 to $300 if you need to request them separately.

No HOA neighborhoods in Valrico avoid these fees entirely, which is one reason [homes without HOA in Valrico](/blog/valrico-homes-with-no-hoa-what-buyers-need-to-know/) have specific appeal to some buyers.

## How Seller Concessions Work in 2026

In Valrico's current 2026 market, **sellers are regularly accepting closing cost contribution requests of 2% to 3% of purchase price** ($8,300 to $12,450 on a $415,000 home). This has become a standard negotiating lever because:

- Buyers are stretched on cash after a down payment
- Sellers who have held since pre-2021 have significant equity and can afford to concede
- Conventional loan guidelines allow seller contributions up to 3% of purchase price (with less than 10% down) or up to 6% (with 10% to 25% down)

**How to structure this correctly:** A seller concession does not reduce the sale price. Instead, the seller credits money to the buyer's closing costs at the HUD settlement statement. This matters because:
- The appraisal must support the purchase price (the concession does not reduce it)
- The concession cannot exceed actual closing costs (you cannot pocket the difference)
- VA loans allow seller concessions up to 4% plus reasonable and customary costs

A concession of $10,000 on a $415,000 transaction essentially allows you to finance your closing costs into the mortgage rather than paying them out of pocket, since you are bringing less cash to closing but borrowing the same amount.

## FHA vs. Conventional vs. VA Closing Cost Differences

Loan type affects closing costs significantly:

**Conventional loan:**
- No upfront mortgage insurance premium (MIP) if you put 20% down
- With less than 20% down, Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) of 0.5% to 1.5%/year is added to your monthly payment
- Lower funding fees than VA

**FHA loan:**
- Upfront Mortgage Insurance Premium (UFMIP): 1.75% of the loan amount, added to loan balance or paid at closing. On a $332,000 loan: $5,810.
- Annual MIP: 0.55% to 0.85%/year depending on loan term and LTV, paid monthly
- Water and septic tests required by lender if applicable
- FHA does allow 100% of closing costs to be covered by seller concessions

**VA loan (for eligible veterans and active duty):**
- No down payment required
- VA Funding Fee: 2.15% of the loan amount for first use with no down payment ($7,138 on a $332,000 loan), typically financed into the loan
- No monthly mortgage insurance
- VA loans allow up to 4% in seller concessions plus customary closing costs
- Lenders are prohibited from charging certain fees under VA loan rules (origination fees capped at 1%)

## Cash to Close: The Number That Actually Matters

Your "cash to close" is the specific dollar amount your title company will tell you to wire two to three days before closing. It includes:

- Down payment
- Closing costs
- Prepaids
- Less any seller concessions or lender credits

**Example for a $415,000 home, conventional 10% down:**
- Down payment: $41,500
- Closing costs: approximately $10,500
- Prepaids (insurance, escrow, prepaid interest): approximately $5,500
- Seller concession (assume 2%): -$8,300
- **Estimated cash to close: approximately $49,200**

That is a meaningful amount of liquid cash. Buyers who have saved enough for the down payment but not the closing costs often find themselves short. This is why I always recommend buyers get a full closing cost estimate from their lender within the first week of searching, not at the end when they are already under contract.

## How to Reduce Your Closing Costs

Several legitimate strategies can reduce what you pay:

1. **Negotiate seller concessions.** In 2026, Valrico sellers are accepting them. Be direct and include them in your initial offer.
2. **Use a lender offering credits in exchange for a slightly higher rate.** If you plan to sell or refinance within five years, a lender credit may save net money compared to a lower rate.
3. **Close at month-end.** Reduces prepaid interest by 20 to 25 days.
4. **Shop closing agent fees.** The title insurance rate is fixed, but settlement fees vary.
5. **Ask your agent about affiliated lender programs.** Some builders and brokerages have preferred lenders who offer closing cost credits to buyers using their financing.
6. **Choose no-HOA neighborhoods.** Avoids HOA transfer and capital contribution fees.
