## What Is a CDD and Why Should You Care?

A Community Development District is a special taxing district that funds infrastructure bonds for a neighborhood's roads, utilities, parks, and amenities. The cost shows up as a non-ad-valorem assessment on your property tax bill — not as a line item you can opt out of or negotiate away. It is there for 20 to 30 years until the bonds are paid off, and even then a reduced assessment may continue for ongoing maintenance.

CDDs are common in newer Florida developments. In the Valrico area, they can add $1,500 to $4,000+ per year to your housing costs. That is $125 to $333 per month that does not build equity, does not reduce your mortgage, and does not go away.

## The Real Financial Impact

On a $450K home at 6.5% with 10% down:

| Monthly Cost | No CDD | With $2,500/yr CDD | With $4,000/yr CDD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mortgage (P&I) | $2,560 | $2,560 | $2,560 |
| Property taxes | $580 | $580 | $580 |
| Insurance | $420 | $420 | $420 |
| CDD | $0 | $208 | $333 |
| **Total** | **$3,560** | **$3,768** | **$3,893** |

That $208 to $333/month CDD assessment over a 30-year mortgage totals $74,880 to $119,880. For context, that is the price of a kitchen and bathroom remodel, a new roof, a pool resurfacing, AND a car — paid to a bond debt instead of your own property.

The purchasing power impact is equally significant. A buyer with a $3,700/month budget can afford a $450K home with no CDD or a $410K home with a $3,000/year CDD. The CDD effectively reduces what you can buy by $30K to $50K.

## How CDDs Differ From HOAs

| Feature | HOA | CDD |
|---|---|---|
| What it funds | Community rules, amenities, common area maintenance | Infrastructure bond repayment |
| Appears on | Separate HOA statement | Property tax bill |
| Can you opt out? | Sometimes (voluntary HOAs) | Never |
| Can it be dissolved? | Yes, by member vote | Not until bonds are repaid |
| Tax deductible? | No (unless rental property) | Partially (as property tax) |
| Duration | Ongoing | 20-30 years, then may reduce |

You can have HOA only, CDD only, both, or neither. The best financial structure for most buyers is neither — and Valrico's established neighborhoods offer exactly that.

## How to Verify CDD Status

The MLS HOA fee field is unreliable for CDD information. Some listings lump CDD into the HOA amount. Others list CDD separately. Some omit it entirely.

The definitive check: pull the full property tax bill from the Hillsborough County Tax Collector website. Non-ad-valorem assessments (including CDD) appear as separate line items below the ad-valorem property taxes. If you see a "Community Development District" charge, the property has a CDD.

## The Investment Angle

For investors, CDD-free properties have a clear advantage: lower carrying costs mean better cash flow. A rental property in Brentwood Hills with no HOA and no CDD has $200 to $400/month less in expenses than a comparable rental in a CDD community. That difference flows directly to your bottom line.

CDD-free properties are also easier to rent because there are no community approval requirements for tenants, no architectural restrictions that complicate maintenance, and no HOA that can change rental policies through board votes.

## The Search Strategy

Because CDD-free properties are concentrated in established neighborhoods, the inventory skews toward older homes (1980s-2000s construction). This means you may need to budget for updates — kitchens, bathrooms, roofs, HVAC. But the ongoing savings from zero CDD often exceed the cost of those improvements within a few years.

**The math:** A $5K kitchen refresh plus $0/year CDD costs less over 5 years than a turnkey newer home with a $3,000/year CDD ($15K in CDD payments alone). The updated older home wins financially.
