## The Rate Environment Entering December 2026

The 30-year conventional mortgage rate touched 7.40% in mid-October 2026 based on Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey. That represents a move up from 6.65% in August and marks the highest sustained rate level since late 2023.

What drove the jump: stronger-than-expected jobs data in September and October shifted market expectations for Federal Reserve rate cuts. The futures market repriced to show fewer and slower reductions through mid-2027. Mortgage rates respond to 10-year Treasury yields, not the overnight Fed funds rate directly, and 10-year yields rose sharply in response.

The practical effect on purchasing power: at 6.65% in August, a buyer with a $2,500 monthly payment could borrow approximately $382,000. At 7.40%, that same payment supports roughly $351,000. A buyer whose budget anchored on a $382,000 home in August now qualifies for $31,000 less -- without any change in income, credit, or down payment. That gap matters for the $340,000 to $420,000 price band that dominates Valrico's 33594 market.

As of mid-November, rates have edged down slightly from the October peak but remain in the 7.10% to 7.25% range. Florida Realtors expects rates to trend toward 6.75% to 7.00% by mid-2027 as the Fed continues a gradual easing cycle, but no dramatic rate drop is projected for the near term.

## Valrico Market Data: What the Numbers Show Entering December

### 33594 ZIP Code (Bloomingdale, Twin Lakes, Copper Ridge, Wellington)

Entering December 2026, the 33594 market reflects a buyer's market across most price tiers.

**Median sale price:** Tracking at approximately $370,000 to $382,000, depending on the two-week window measured. This represents essentially flat year-over-year performance -- a meaningful outcome given the broader Hillsborough County softening and the highest sustained mortgage rates in 24 months.

**Price per square foot:** Standard 3-to-4-bedroom single-family homes are closing at $175 to $195 per square foot. Well-maintained Bloomingdale homes with recent roofs and updated kitchens are holding the high end of that range. Dated homes or homes with deferred maintenance are clearing in the $160 to $175 range.

**Active listings:** Approximately 140 to 160 active single-family homes in 33594. This is a meaningful increase from the 90 to 110 range that characterized the tight 2022-2023 period.

**Days on market:** 50 to 68 days on average across all listings. Move-in-ready, correctly priced homes in the $340,000 to $400,000 range are still moving in 25 to 40 days. Overpriced listings are sitting 90 to 120 days before sellers make meaningful price adjustments.

**Price reductions:** Approximately 37% of active 33594 listings have taken at least one price reduction. The average reduction among homes that have cut is approximately $12,000 to $18,000 from original list price. This is not a panic market, but it is a market where original pricing ambition rarely survives contact with reality.

**Sale-to-list ratio:** 96.5% to 97% across completed transactions. Sellers are not capitulating on price at the contract stage, but are offering closing cost concessions averaging $6,000 to $10,000. The practical effect is a sale-to-net ratio closer to 94.5% to 95.5% when concessions are factored in.

The school zone demand insulates sellers from the degree of price pressure seen in comparable Hillsborough County neighborhoods without strong school zone adjacency.

## The Rate Lock Effect: Why Supply Is Not Surging

One statistic that puzzles some buyers: inventory is elevated compared to 2022-2023, but it is not at the levels you would expect if homeowners were freely moving.

The reason is the rate lock effect. Approximately 72% of existing Florida homeowners with mortgages hold loans at rates below 4.5%, per data from the Mortgage Bankers Association. A homeowner who purchased a Valrico home in 2020 at 2.75% faces a stark choice: sell a $420,000 home with a $1,650 monthly principal and interest payment, and buy a comparable home at 7.40% with a $2,615 monthly payment on the same loan balance. The effective monthly payment increase is $965, or $11,580 per year.

That math keeps many potential move-up sellers in place. The supply that is hitting the market is primarily from genuine life events: job relocation, divorce, estate sales, downsizing, and new construction from builders who have no choice but to sell.

This dynamic also means that when rates eventually drop to the 5.5% to 6.0% range -- which most economists project for 2028-2029 -- the inventory surge will be significant as pent-up sellers finally move. Buyers purchasing at today's higher rates with the ability to refinance will be in a strong position when that happens.

## Foreclosure and Distress Activity: What the Data Shows

Valrico and east Hillsborough County are not experiencing a foreclosure crisis entering December 2026. The indicators that typically precede a wave -- rising delinquency rates, unemployment spikes, and negative equity -- are not present in this ZIP code market.

Hillsborough County public records show approximately 180 to 220 active foreclosure filings countywide, a modest increase from the 120 to 150 range that characterized the 2024-2025 period but well below the 1,200 to 1,800 range seen annually during the 2008 to 2012 downturn. Valrico 33594 and 33596 specifically represent a small fraction of that countywide filing activity.

Distressed property sales (REO, short sales, and pre-foreclosure) account for less than 3% of total Valrico transactions in Q4 2026, compared to 28% to 35% during the 2010 to 2013 recovery period. This is a healthy, if slow, resale market. It is not a distressed market.

The homeowners most at risk are those who purchased with minimal down payment in 2022 to 2023 at peak pricing and who have experienced job disruption. Valrico's employment base in Hillsborough County, anchored by healthcare, logistics, distribution, and MacDill AFB civilian and military employment, has remained relatively resilient.

## What Buyers Should Do in December 2026

**Act on leverage, not on waiting for lower rates.** Buyers who delay in search of a 5.5% rate may find themselves competing in a spring 2027 market with more competition and sellers who have recalibrated pricing expectations upward. The buyers who purchased in December with seller-paid rate buydowns and year-end concessions are likely to look back favorably on that timing.

**Ask for seller-paid rate buydowns.** At 7.40% rates, sellers in Valrico are increasingly open to contributing $8,000 to $12,000 at closing to buy down the buyer's rate. A 1-0 buydown reduces the buyer's rate by 1% in year one (effective 6.40% on a 7.40% note), saving approximately $175 per month on a $380,000 mortgage in year one. A 2-1 buydown reduces the rate by 2% in year one and 1% in year two, with the full rate taking effect in year three. These are negotiable and common in December 2026 Valrico transactions.

**Use the holiday slowdown to your advantage.** Buyer competition drops 30 to 40% in December versus September and October. Sellers who are still listed in December are genuinely motivated. Many Valrico sellers have been listed for 60 to 90 days and are carrying the full ownership cost through the holidays. That motivation is negotiating capital.

**Get pre-approved, not just pre-qualified.** In December's market, sellers are accepting fewer offers before choosing. A fully underwritten pre-approval from a local lender with the ability to close in 25 to 30 days is a meaningful offer differentiator.

**Budget for property taxes accurately.** Amendment 3 passed November 4, 2026, expanding the non-school homestead exemption from $25,000 to $150,000 effective January 1, 2027. A buyer who closes before December 31, 2026 and establishes homestead exemption on a $420,000 Valrico home will see approximately $1,100 in annual property tax savings compared to the pre-Amendment 3 baseline. That savings compounds for every year of ownership.

## What Sellers Should Do in December 2026

**Price to the current market, not the spring peak.** The 33594 and 33596 markets peaked in Q1-Q2 2026 at median values approximately $15,000 to $25,000 above current trading ranges. Sellers who list at spring 2026 pricing are contributing to the 37% price-reduction statistic. Homes priced at or slightly below the current median for their condition and location are still moving in 25 to 45 days.

**Offer seller concessions proactively.** At 7.40% rates, buyers face elevated monthly payments. A seller who proactively offers $8,000 to $10,000 in closing cost credits in the listing presentation -- or structures a rate buydown contribution -- expands the qualified buyer pool and reduces negotiation friction. The alternative is a price reduction after 45 days on market, which typically costs more than a proactive concession.

**Target a year-end close.** Sellers who go under contract in December with a December 31 or early January close are transacting with buyers who are motivated to close before the homestead exemption and tax year deadline. That buyer motivation is genuine leverage for sellers seeking to close quickly and cleanly.

**Stage and present well.** December competition is lower for sellers too -- fewer listings to compare against. A well-presented home in correct condition stands out in December more than it would in April or May when buyers have 15 comparable listings to tour.

## The 2027 Outlook: What December 2026 Buyers and Sellers Need to Know

The 2027 Valrico market outlook hinges primarily on three variables: mortgage rate trajectory, Hillsborough County employment, and Amendment 3 implementation.

**Rate trajectory:** Most credible forecasters project 30-year rates in the 6.75% to 7.00% range for Q1-Q2 2027, with modest easing into the 6.50% range possible by mid-2027 if inflation continues tracking downward. No scenario currently visible in the data produces a return to the 5% range before 2028 at the earliest. Buyers waiting for dramatically lower rates in 2027 are likely to be disappointed.

**Amendment 3 impact:** The expanded homestead exemption takes effect January 1, 2027 for anyone who establishes homestead by March 1, 2027. The 33594 market will see annual tax savings of approximately $950 to $1,050 per homeowner. The 33596 market, with higher assessed values, will see approximately $1,050 to $1,150 in annual savings. This modestly improves affordability and may bring some fence-sitting sellers into the market in Q1 2027 who want to sell before the spring listing surge.

**Employment and demographic demand:** Hillsborough County population growth continues despite elevated mortgage rates. The underlying demand for housing in the Newsome High School zone, in Bloomingdale's established walkable neighborhood character, and in east Hillsborough County's proximity to both Tampa employment and I-4 corridor jobs remains durable. Valrico does not have the speculative investor or short-term rental overlay that makes other Florida markets more volatile.

The most likely 2027 scenario for Valrico: a stable market with modest appreciation of 2% to 4% by mid-2027 as rates edge down and Amendment 3 savings take effect. Not a boom. Not a bust. A buyer's market transitioning gradually toward balance as the year progresses.
