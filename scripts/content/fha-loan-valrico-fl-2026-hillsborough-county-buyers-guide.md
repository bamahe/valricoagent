## What Is an FHA Loan and Who Should Use One in Valrico FL

An FHA loan is a mortgage insured by the Federal Housing Administration, a division of the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). Because the government backs the loan, lenders offer FHA financing to borrowers with lower credit scores and smaller down payments than conventional loans typically require.

FHA is not a government handout. It is a financing structure where the borrower pays mortgage insurance premiums (MIP) to compensate for the risk the government absorbs. The tradeoff is straightforward: access more purchasing power now in exchange for a higher all-in monthly cost.

For Valrico buyers in 2026, FHA makes sense in three specific scenarios:

- You have a 580 or higher credit score but cannot pull together a 20 percent down payment on a Valrico home priced at $370,000 to $470,000
- You are a first-time buyer purchasing a primary residence and want to get into the market now rather than waiting to save a larger down payment while home prices and rents both remain elevated
- Your credit score is in the 500 to 579 range and you can bring 10 percent down -- conventional lending typically requires 620 or higher at minimum, and many conventional lenders in Hillsborough County prefer 660 or above

FHA does not work for second homes, investment properties, or homes that fail FHA minimum property standards (more on that below).

## FHA Down Payment Requirements

FHA down payment requirements are credit-score dependent:

**Credit score 580 and above:** 3.5 percent down payment required.
**Credit score 500 to 579:** 10 percent down payment required.
**Credit score below 500:** Not eligible for FHA financing.

Most lenders impose their own "overlays" -- minimum score requirements above FHA's floor. In practice, many Hillsborough County lenders require a 580 minimum to approve an FHA loan at 3.5 percent down, and some set their floor at 620. If your score is in the 580 to 619 range, compare lenders carefully or work with a mortgage broker who has access to multiple FHA-approved lenders.

## FHA Mortgage Insurance Premiums

Here is where FHA buyers pay the real cost of the program. FHA requires two types of mortgage insurance:

### Upfront MIP (UFMIP)

**1.75 percent of the total loan amount.** This is charged at closing but is typically rolled into the loan balance rather than paid out of pocket.

- On a $364,770 loan: UFMIP = $6,383, bringing the financed balance to $371,153
- On a $450,655 loan: UFMIP = $7,886, bringing the financed balance to $458,541
- On a $501,800 loan: UFMIP = $8,782, bringing the financed balance to $510,582

### Annual MIP

Charged monthly based on the outstanding loan balance. For a standard 30-year FHA loan with less than 10 percent down, the annual MIP rate is typically approximately **0.55 percent of the loan balance**.

- Monthly MIP on $371,153: $371,153 x 0.0055 / 12 = approximately **$170 per month**
- Monthly MIP on $458,541: $458,541 x 0.0055 / 12 = approximately **$210 per month**

### How Long Does MIP Last?

This is the most important FHA cost factor for long-term planning:

- If you put **less than 10 percent down**, MIP is permanent for the life of the 30-year loan. It does NOT automatically cancel when you reach 20 percent equity, unlike conventional PMI.
- If you put **10 percent or more down**, MIP cancels after 11 years.

The permanent MIP is the main reason buyers refinance out of FHA into a conventional loan once they have built sufficient equity. Many Valrico buyers who close FHA today anticipate refinancing within 3 to 5 years as rates eventually moderate, at which point the MIP issue is resolved.

Barrett's view: "Most FHA buyers I work with in Valrico understand the MIP is a temporary cost. They are getting into the Newsome zone now, building equity, and planning to refinance when rates come down. The 3.5 percent down payment gets them into the right neighborhood for their family. The long-term numbers still work."

## FHA Minimum Property Standards: What This Means for Valrico Buyers

FHA appraisals are more rigorous than conventional appraisals. The FHA appraiser evaluates both market value and habitability. Properties with the following issues often do not pass:

- Peeling or chipping paint, particularly on homes built before 1978 (Valrico has significant pre-1990 inventory in 33594 where this is common)
- Missing or non-functional systems: HVAC, water heater, roof with active leaks
- Broken windows, doors that do not latch, damaged flooring
- Standing water or evidence of active moisture intrusion
- Exposed wiring or non-functional electrical panels
- Safety hazards including missing handrails on stairs

Homes built before 1978 also trigger a mandatory lead paint disclosure. Much of Valrico's pre-1990 housing stock in 33594 falls into this category.

The practical implication for Valrico buyers: FHA works extremely well on homes built after 1990 that have been reasonably maintained. For older homes, distressed properties, or homes with known deferred maintenance, the FHA appraisal can create complications or require repairs before closing. If you are considering a home that needs significant work, conventional financing may be more flexible.

For sellers with FHA-eligible homes in good condition, noting in MLS remarks that the property is "FHA-eligible" can expand the buyer pool meaningfully. In the current Valrico market, that additional buyer exposure helps.

## FHA vs VA vs Conventional: Which Loan Fits Your Situation

Barrett works with buyers using all three major loan types. Here is the simplified comparison for a Valrico purchase:

**FHA:** Best for buyers with 580 to 740 credit scores, limited down payment (3.5%), primary residence purchase. Permanent MIP is the cost. Accepts credit scores conventional lenders decline. Best for buyers who expect to refinance within 5 to 7 years.

**VA (Veterans Affairs):** Available to veterans, active-duty military, and eligible surviving spouses. No down payment required. No monthly mortgage insurance. The best loan structure available to eligible Valrico buyers. REMAX Collective regularly works with MacDill AFB-affiliated buyers using VA financing in both 33594 and 33596. See the [Valrico VA loan guide](/blog/va-loan-home-buying-valrico-fl-macdill-afb-guide) for the full breakdown.

**Conventional:** Best for buyers with 20 percent down (avoid PMI entirely) or 10 percent down and strong credit (PMI cancels at 20 percent equity). Higher credit score requirements. No property condition requirements beyond basic safety. Most flexible for investment and second-home purchases.

**USDA rural development:** Not applicable to Valrico proper, which falls within the Tampa-Brandon metropolitan area. Some properties on the far eastern edges of Hillsborough County, toward the Plant City and Zephyrhills corridors, may fall within USDA-eligible rural zones.

**What is the FHA loan limit for Hillsborough County FL in 2026?**
The 2026 FHA single-family loan limit for Hillsborough County is $541,287. This covers the majority of Valrico's active listing inventory in both 33594 and 33596 ZIP codes. The 2027 limits were not yet published as of November 2026 -- check HUD's site for updates in December 2026 or January 2027.

**Can I use an FHA loan to buy in the Newsome High School zone in Valrico 33596?**
Yes. Homes priced under $541,287 in 33596 -- which includes much of the Buckhorn, River Hills, and Diamond Hill inventory in the $400,000 to $530,000 range -- are FHA-eligible by loan size. The FHA appraisal and minimum property standards still apply, so home condition matters. Newer construction (post-2000) in the Newsome zone typically sails through FHA appraisal.

**Will FHA mortgage insurance eventually go away on my Valrico home?**
Only if you put 10 percent or more down at closing, in which case annual MIP cancels after 11 years. If you put 3.5 percent down, MIP is permanent unless you refinance into a conventional loan. Many buyers in Valrico plan to refinance when rates moderate in the coming years.

**How does an FHA loan compare to a conventional loan for a first-time buyer in Valrico?**
FHA requires less down payment (3.5 percent vs typically 5 to 20 percent for conventional) and accepts lower credit scores. The tradeoff is FHA mortgage insurance, which adds approximately $170 to $210 per month to a typical Valrico purchase and is permanent unless you refinance. Conventional PMI, by contrast, cancels automatically when you reach 20 percent equity.

**Are there Valrico neighborhoods where homes are too expensive for FHA?**
River Hills Country Club (33596) and the luxury sections of Diamond Hill have homes priced above the $541,287 FHA limit, so those require conventional or jumbo financing. The majority of Valrico's inventory remains well within FHA range.

**What credit score do I need for an FHA loan in Hillsborough County?**
HUD sets the minimum at 500, but lenders in Hillsborough County typically require 580 for the 3.5 percent down option. Some lenders set their own floor at 620. If your score is in the 580 to 620 range, work with a mortgage broker who can shop multiple lenders rather than going to a single bank.
