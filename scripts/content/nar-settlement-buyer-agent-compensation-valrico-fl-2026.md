## What the NAR Settlement Actually Changed

The National Association of Realtors reached a settlement agreement in March 2024 that took effect August 17, 2024. The two core rules that changed real estate transactions in Florida and nationwide:

**Rule 1: Written buyer-broker agreements are required before touring.** Any buyer working with a Realtor must sign a written agreement before touring a home, including virtual tours. The agreement must state how the buyer's agent will be compensated, in terms that are specific and not open-ended.

**Rule 2: Offers of buyer's agent compensation were removed from MLS.** Prior to August 2024, sellers routinely offered a specific percentage to the buyer's agent as part of the MLS listing. That MLS field is gone. Buyer's agent compensation is now a separate negotiation, handled through the purchase contract.

That is the whole settlement in its simplest form. What it means in practice is more nuanced.

## What It Looks Like in a Valrico Transaction Today

Before the settlement, a typical east Hillsborough County transaction worked like this: seller agrees to pay total commission of 5 to 6 percent at closing, with roughly half going to the buyer's agent. This was disclosed in the MLS so any buyer's agent knew upfront what they would earn.

Today, that same deal works like this: the buyer and their agent sign a buyer-broker agreement specifying the agent's compensation. The buyer then makes an offer on the home. The offer can include a request for the seller to contribute toward the buyer's agent fee as part of seller concessions. The seller can accept, reject, or counter that request.

In practice across Valrico and east Hillsborough County, most sellers in 2026 are still offering buyer's agent compensation, just not through the MLS. It now appears in the purchase contract as a seller concession, typically 2 to 3 percent of the purchase price. The economics have not changed dramatically for most transactions. What changed is the transparency and the paperwork.

The key difference: a buyer whose agent charges more than what a seller is willing to contribute can now be asked to pay the gap out of pocket. That was essentially impossible under the old system where the seller always covered the full buyer's agent fee.

## Florida-Specific Rules on Top of the NAR Settlement

Florida's brokerage relationship laws add a layer on top of the NAR settlement rules that buyers and sellers in Valrico should understand.

**Transaction broker is the default in Florida.** Unlike many states where agents act as exclusive buyer's agents with full fiduciary duties, Florida licensees operate as transaction brokers by default. A transaction broker owes limited representation to both parties, not full fiduciary duty to either. If a buyer or seller wants full single-agent representation, they must request it in writing.

**Single-agent designation changes the relationship.** A buyer who wants their agent to have a fiduciary duty to them, not just facilitate the transaction, must sign a single-agent disclosure. This is separate from the written buyer-broker agreement the NAR settlement requires. Many Valrico buyers skip this and get transaction broker service, which is fine for routine transactions but matters in complex negotiations.

**The compensation disclosure requirement.** Under Florida law, licensees must disclose how they are being compensated and by whom. The post-settlement buyer-broker agreement satisfies this requirement for buyers. Sellers receive similar disclosure in the listing agreement.

## What Valrico Buyers Need to Know Before Signing a Buyer-Broker Agreement

A buyer-broker agreement is a contract. Before you sign one, understand what you are agreeing to.

**Compensation structure.** The agreement must state how much the buyer's agent earns. This can be expressed as a flat fee, a percentage of the purchase price, or an hourly rate. Most agents in the Valrico market use a percentage, typically 2.5 to 3 percent of the purchase price. On a $413,000 home, 3 percent is $12,390.

**Duration.** The agreement has a term, typically 90 days or until closing on a home. Some agreements allow you to work with other agents if you tour properties independently during that period. Read the exclusivity clause carefully.

**What happens if the seller does not offer to cover it.** If you make an offer on a home and the seller will not contribute to your agent's compensation, your buyer-broker agreement specifies whether you owe your agent the difference. This is the scenario that generated the most confusion after August 2024. Clarify this before you tour anything.

**Termination clause.** You should be able to end the relationship if the agent is not performing. Look for a termination clause that lets either party exit with reasonable notice.

## What Valrico Sellers Should Know About Buyer's Agent Compensation

The settlement eliminated the requirement that sellers offer compensation to the buyer's agent. You are not required to offer anything. But strategy matters.

**Most sellers are still offering 2 to 2.5 percent.** The practical reason is buyer pool size. When a buyer's agent searches MLS for homes, their buyer has already signed an agreement specifying what that agent earns. If your home's offer does not include seller-paid compensation and the buyer cannot or will not pay the gap, that buyer may not make an offer on your home. Sellers who offer competitive buyer's agent compensation attract more buyer traffic.

**How to offer it.** You can authorize your listing agent to disclose in MLS remarks, in showing instructions, or in direct agent-to-agent communication that seller-paid compensation is available. It cannot appear in the MLS compensation fields, which no longer exist. Many Valrico listing agents use showing instruction notes or Showing Time notes to communicate this.

**You can negotiate it at offer time.** Compensation offered to the buyer's agent does not have to be pre-committed. You can evaluate each offer on its own terms, including whether the buyer has requested seller-paid compensation, and decide what to offer. This flexibility is new compared to the pre-settlement system.

**Including it in the net sheet.** When Barrett prepares a seller net sheet for a Valrico listing, he includes expected buyer's agent compensation as a line item, just as it always was. For most listings in the $380,000 to $500,000 range, that is $9,500 to $15,000 of the total transaction cost.

## Commission Rates: What Is Actually Happening in Valrico

The prediction that the NAR settlement would dramatically reduce agent commission rates has largely not materialized in the Valrico market. Industry data from multiple sources suggests total commission rates have declined modestly, perhaps 0.2 to 0.4 percentage points on average nationally, rather than the dramatic reductions some predicted.

In east Hillsborough County, the dynamics reflect a relatively thin agent market where experienced local agents can demonstrate value and buyers competing for homes in the Newsome High School zone are still motivated to work with competent representation. Commission compression is more visible in higher-price-point markets with many competing agents than in Valrico's mid-market price range.

What has changed more than commission rates is buyer awareness. Buyers in Valrico today understand that agent compensation is negotiable in a way that buyers in 2022 did not. That is a healthy market development.

## How to Choose a Buyer's Agent Under the New Rules

The buyer-broker agreement is an opportunity to have a conversation you should always have had before the settlement: what exactly are you getting for the compensation you are paying?

Questions to ask a Valrico buyer's agent before signing:

- How do you determine the right offer price for a Valrico home in 2026, with 30 to 35 percent of active listings having taken price reductions?
- What is your experience with Newsome High School zone properties versus homes in 33594?
- If the seller does not cover your compensation, what happens to the agreed fee?
- How many Valrico transactions have you closed in the last 12 months?
- What do you do differently from an agent I could find through an online matching service?

The last question matters. National online matching services and discount models have proliferated since the settlement. The difference in Valrico is that neighborhood-specific comp knowledge, relationships with other local agents, and familiarity with specific subdivision nuances are real and demonstrable advantages. Ask an agent to demonstrate them before you sign.
