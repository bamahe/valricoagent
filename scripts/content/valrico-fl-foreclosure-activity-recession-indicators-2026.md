Every few months a headline claims Florida is "on the brink of a housing crash." Usually the headline is wrong, but it's rarely wrong for no reason at all - there are real signals worth watching, and Valrico isn't immune to them. As someone who works this market every day in 33594 and 33596, I wanted to put the actual numbers on the table: what's happening with foreclosures in Hillsborough County, what the classic recession indicators (inventory, days on market, price cuts) are doing locally, and what any of it actually means if you own a home here or are thinking about buying one.

Short version: this is not 2008. But it also isn't 2021 anymore, and homeowners and buyers who plan around outdated assumptions are the ones who get surprised.

## Where the Valrico Market Actually Stands Right Now

Start with the numbers that matter most if you're pricing a listing or writing an offer.

- **Median sale price:** Valrico's median sale price is around $413,000, down roughly 6% year-over-year.
- **Price per square foot:** The median is about $188/sq ft, down 6.9% from a year ago - a meaningful compression, not a rounding error.
- **Days on market:** Listings are averaging in the mid-70s across Valrico, with 33596 specifically at 73 days and 33594 running similarly. Compare that to the 47-54 day averages the broader Tampa Bay region was seeing just a year or two ago.
- **Inventory:** Roughly 172 homes are listed across Valrico, and 33596 is sitting at about 2.0 months of supply - still tilted toward sellers, but a long way from the sub-one-month supply that defined 2021-2022.
- **Offers per listing:** Homes here are averaging around 2 offers, not the 10-plus bidding wars of the pandemic years.

None of these numbers, on their own, scream "recession." But together they describe a market that has cooled and re-priced - which is exactly what the wider Tampa Bay data confirms.

## The Recession Indicators, Zoomed Out to Tampa Bay

Valrico doesn't move independently of Tampa Bay, so it's worth looking at the metro-level trend that's pulling it along:

- Prices are down across the board. Tampa Bay's median single-family price has fallen roughly 3.6% year-over-year, with condos and townhomes hit even harder - down as much as 12% in some reporting.
- More than half of active listings have taken a price cut. That's a level of negotiating leverage buyers haven't had locally in years.
- Homes are selling below list. Recent closings in the metro are averaging roughly 4% under original list price.
- Inventory has more than doubled from the bottom. Months-of-supply has climbed from around 2 months in 2022 to roughly 5.2-5.4 months in 2026 - a swing of that size is the single clearest market-has-shifted signal in real estate data.
- Time on market has stretched. Metro-wide days on market are now running 61-84 days depending on the data source, up from the 47-54 day range a year prior.

These are the textbook indicators analysts watch for signs of a slowing housing cycle: rising supply, longer marketing times, shrinking prices, and sellers conceding ground. Valrico's own numbers track this pattern closely.

## Foreclosure Activity: What's Actually Happening in Hillsborough County

This is the number people ask about most, and it deserves a careful answer rather than a scary headline.

Nationally, ATTOM Data Solutions' mid-year 2026 U.S. Foreclosure Market Report recorded 227,548 properties with a foreclosure filing in the first half of the year - up 21% from the same period in 2025 and up 28% compared to 2024. Florida posted one of the highest state-level foreclosure rates in the country during this period, alongside South Carolina and Indiana ([source: HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/us-foreclosures-rise-2026-midyear-attom-report/)).

At the county level, ATTOM's own Hillsborough County data currently shows 183 properties in some stage of foreclosure and 4 bank-owned (REO) properties ([source: ATTOM Data Solutions](https://www.attomdata.com/data/us-real-estate/fl/hillsborough-county/)). For context, Hillsborough County has roughly 550,000 housing units, so even 183 active foreclosure filings represents a very small slice of the overall market - nowhere near the volume seen during the 2008-2011 crisis.

A word of caution: several lead-generation sites advertise dramatically higher "foreclosure listing" counts for Valrico specifically. Those figures typically aggregate expired pre-foreclosure notices, stale MLS data, and properties that never actually reach auction, and they should be treated skeptically. ATTOM's filing data and the [Hillsborough County Clerk of Court's foreclosure sale calendar](https://www.hillsclerk.com/court-services/foreclosure-sales) are the more reliable sources.

The honest read: foreclosure activity is rising off a very low post-pandemic base, both nationally and in Hillsborough County, and it's a data point worth watching over the next few quarters - particularly if rates stay elevated and insurance costs keep squeezing local carrying costs. But at 183 active county-wide filings, it is not yet a Valrico-specific distress signal. It's a slow normalization, not a crisis.

## What the High End of the Market Is Telling Us

One reason it's easy to overstate the "recession" narrative is that the top of the Valrico market is still performing. High-end sales - including recent closings above $975,000 in Bloomingdale - coexist with softer days-on-market and price-per-square-foot numbers because this market isn't moving uniformly. Well-priced, well-maintained homes in strong school zones are still transacting quickly and at strong prices, while overpriced or dated listings absorb the extra 20-30 days on market and the price cuts. For the full quarterly breakdown of what's selling and what's sitting, see the [current Valrico market report](/valrico-market-report/).

## What This Means If You're Selling in Valrico Right Now

Price to today's comps, not last year's. With price-per-square-foot down nearly 7% year-over-year, pulling comps from 2025 will overprice your listing and add unnecessary days on market.

Expect negotiation. More than half of active Tampa Bay listings have already cut price at least once. Building in some cushion, or pricing tightly from day one, beats pricing high and chasing the market down.

Condition and staging matter more in a 5-month-supply market than they did in a 1-month-supply market. Buyers have choices again. If you want a specific number for your property, our [Valrico seller's guide](/sell-my-home-valrico/) walks through how we price listings in this environment.

## What This Means If You're Buying in Valrico Right Now

You have real leverage for the first time in years - more inventory, longer average marketing times, and sellers who are more willing to negotiate on price, closing costs, or repairs.

Distressed inventory is not yet a significant opportunity here. With only 183 active foreclosure filings across the entire county, don't expect a wave of steeply discounted bank-owned properties in Valrico specifically. Most of what shows up under "foreclosure" search filters on aggregator sites is stale or already resolved. See our [Valrico foreclosures page](/valrico-foreclosures/) for what's actually listed.

Rate environment still matters more than headlines. Your monthly payment is far more sensitive to mortgage rates than to the difference between a 2-month and a 5-month supply market. Get pre-approved and know your real number before you start touring.

## The Bottom Line

Valrico's market has clearly cooled from the frenzy of 2021-2022: prices and price-per-square-foot are down modestly, inventory has grown substantially, and homes are taking longer to sell. Those are genuine, data-confirmed indicators consistent with what's happening across Tampa Bay and much of Florida. Foreclosure activity is rising too, both nationally and in Hillsborough County - but from such a low base that it remains a trend to monitor, not a crisis unfolding in Valrico neighborhoods today.
