## What Florida's HB 1203 Actually Changed for Homeowners

The 2024 law, now codified as Chapter 2024-221 in Florida statutes, operates on three tracks: strengthening homeowner rights against arbitrary HOA restrictions, creating transparency and accountability requirements for HOA boards and managers, and setting criminal penalties for financial misconduct.

Here is what matters most for buyers evaluating an HOA in east Hillsborough County.

### New Homeowner Rights Against Arbitrary Restrictions

**Vegetable gardens and clotheslines.** Prior to July 1, 2024, an HOA could prohibit you from planting vegetables in your yard or hanging laundry on a clothesline. The 2024 law changed that. HOAs are now prohibited from restricting vegetable gardens or clotheslines that are located in areas not visible from the street frontage or adjacent parcels. If your HOA docs still contain a ban on vegetable gardens that pre-dates 2024, the state law overrides it.

**Interior spaces.** The law prohibits HOAs and their architectural review committees from regulating or placing requirements on the interior of a home that cannot be seen from the street. This matters for buyers who want to renovate interiors -- the HOA's architectural committee has no jurisdiction over what it cannot observe from public view.

**Parking protections.** The law includes new parking rights, with specific protections for first responders and certain work vehicles. Prior to 2024, some HOAs were issuing fines or towing vehicles belonging to police officers, firefighters, and emergency medical personnel who parked their work vehicles at their homes. The 2024 law restricts those prohibitions.

**Holiday decoration fining.** HOAs are no longer permitted to fine a homeowner for holiday decorations without first providing written advance notice and a one-week cure period to remove the items. This is a narrow but practically meaningful protection given the number of HOA disputes that have involved holiday lights in Florida.

**Architectural denial must cite specific rules.** When an HOA denies an architectural or improvement request, the association must provide a written notice stating specifically which rule or covenant provision the request violates. Vague or conclusory denials are no longer sufficient under state law. This matters if you plan to build a fence, add a shed, convert a garage, or make any exterior modification.

## Transparency Requirements: What HOAs Must Now Disclose

The 2024 law imposes significant new transparency requirements on HOAs in Florida, with the most demanding obligations applying to larger associations.

**Online records posting.** An HOA with 100 or more parcels is now required to post its official records on the association's website or a dedicated application by January 1, 2025. Official records include the declaration of covenants, articles of incorporation, bylaws, rules and regulations, the current budget, most recent annual financial report, board meeting minutes for the past three years, and contracts with vendors. This deadline has already passed, so if you are looking at a qualifying HOA community that cannot produce a website with these documents, the association is out of compliance.

**Updates within 14 business days.** Once posted, the records must be updated within 14 business days of any change. If the board adopts a rule amendment, that amendment must appear on the website within two weeks.

**Community association managers.** CAMs are now required to provide a copy of their management contract to any homeowner upon request. If a buyer wants to understand what a management company is being paid and what services are included, that right is now codified.

**Law enforcement records access.** Associations must provide records copies or make them available within five business days of a law enforcement subpoena. This is a governance provision, but it reflects the legislature's recognition that some Florida HOAs had been uncooperative with legitimate investigations.

## Anti-Corruption Provisions: Criminal Penalties for HOA Misconduct

The most consequential governance change in HB 1203 is the creation of criminal penalties for financial misconduct by HOA officers, directors, and community association managers.

If an HOA officer, director, or manager accepts a kickback from a vendor in connection with an HOA contract, that act is now a criminal offense under Florida law. Florida has had a documented history of HOA-related fraud, particularly in larger communities where the association controls significant maintenance contracts. The 2024 law recognizes this as a law enforcement matter, not just a civil dispute.

The law also addresses fraudulent voting in HOA elections, which had become a persistent source of HOA litigation in Florida. Association elections must follow procedures designed to prevent manipulation of ballots or proxy votes.

For buyers, these provisions matter because they go to the financial integrity of the HOA you are joining. When you buy into an HOA community, you are inheriting that community's financial health and its exposure to past mismanagement.

Valrico's most common HOA communities include Bloomingdale, Buckhorn Preserve, Diamond Hill, River Hills, Canterbury Club, Copper Ridge, and several smaller subdivisions. Each has its own declaration, rules, and fee structure. The 2024 law applies to all of them.

Bloomingdale has various HOA sections with different levels of governance. Some sections are voluntary, some have mandatory HOAs. ## HOA Due Diligence Checklist for Valrico Buyers in 2026-2027

Understanding the law is the foundation. Here is the practical checklist buyers should work through before closing on an HOA property:

**1. Request and review the governing documents early.** In Florida, the HOA must provide you with its declaration, bylaws, articles of incorporation, and rules and regulations within a set period after you go under contract. Do not skip this step. These documents define what you can and cannot do with your property for as long as you own it.

**2. Review the most recent budget and reserve study.** The HOA's budget tells you what it costs to run the community. The reserve study tells you whether the association has been setting aside adequate funds for future capital expenses like roof replacements, pool resurfacing, and pavement. Underfunded reserves mean special assessments.

**3. Check for pending or recent special assessments.** Ask directly whether any special assessments have been levied in the past 24 months or are currently being considered. Special assessments can run $1,000 to $10,000 or more per homeowner for major capital projects.

**4. Review the meeting minutes.** Under the 2024 law, associations must post the last three years of board meeting minutes online. Reading through meeting minutes gives you an unfiltered picture of the issues the community is actually dealing with: roof disputes, vendor problems, delinquency rates, and pending litigation.

**5. Verify delinquency rates.** An HOA with high delinquency rates on dues collection has a revenue problem. That problem often leads to special assessments on homeowners who do pay. The reserve study and financial report should include delinquency data.

**6. Understand the fee increase history.** Ask what the monthly fee was three years ago and what it is today. HOA fees that have increased significantly without a corresponding increase in services or reserves suggest financial mismanagement.

**7. Check for pending litigation.** HOA litigation against developers, contractors, or homeowners creates contingent liabilities that could affect the association's financial stability. Some lenders will not finance a home in an HOA that has significant pending litigation.

## HOA vs. No-HOA: The Valrico Buyer's Choice

Not all Valrico communities have HOAs, and that is one of the market's real advantages over newer master-planned communities. Some buyers in Valrico specifically seek out non-HOA properties for the freedom they provide.

Non-HOA neighborhoods in Valrico include sections of Valrico Forest, parts of the older Bloomingdale subdivisions, and scattered homes on larger rural parcels near Lumsden Road and Bell Shoals. For buyers who want to park a boat or RV, run a home-based business, or simply avoid monthly dues, non-HOA Valrico offers options that do not exist in communities like Wesley Chapel or FishHawk Ranch.

That said, non-HOA properties carry their own risks. Without deed restrictions, neighboring properties can be used in ways that affect your quality of life and property value. Many buyers want the consistency that an HOA provides, and the 2024 law makes that trade-off less one-sided than it used to be.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Florida HOA Reform 2024

**Can my Valrico HOA still tell me what color to paint my house?**
Yes. The 2024 law did not eliminate HOA authority over exterior appearance, landscaping visible from the street, or most traditional architectural controls. It carved out specific protections for interior spaces, vegetable gardens, clotheslines, and certain vehicles. Your HOA still has broad authority over exterior appearance.

**Does the 2024 HOA reform apply to condo associations?**
HB 1203 is specifically focused on homeowners' associations. Florida's condo law (Chapter 718) was amended separately by SB 4-D (2022) and additional legislation following the Surfside collapse. The laws overlap in some areas but are distinct statutes.

**What can I do if my HOA is violating the 2024 law?**
Homeowners can file a complaint with the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR), which oversees community association managers and enforces certain HOA law provisions. For violations involving fines, architectural denials, or records requests, an HOA dispute attorney can often resolve the issue through a demand letter before litigation becomes necessary.

**How do I find out if a Valrico HOA is in compliance with the online records posting requirement?**
Ask the listing agent for the HOA's website or app link. If the HOA qualifies (100 or more parcels) and cannot provide a working link to current governing documents, that is a compliance gap. You can also contact the HOA management company directly.
