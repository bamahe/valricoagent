If you are shopping the east Hillsborough and SouthShore corridor and comparing Valrico against Apollo Beach, you are asking a question more buyers are raising in 2026. Both communities sit within Hillsborough County, both are within reasonable commute distance of Tampa, and both offer suburban single-family neighborhoods away from the density of South Tampa. But the similarities end there. What you get for your money, your insurance exposure, your school options, and your day-to-day lifestyle are meaningfully different between these two markets. Here is a straight comparison based on current Q4 2026 data so you can make an informed decision.

## Price: What You Are Actually Paying

**Valrico** operates across two ZIP codes with distinct price tiers:

- **33594 (Bloomingdale area):** Median sold price $370,000 to $382,000 entering Q4 2026, with days on market averaging 50 to 68 days and roughly 240 to 260 active listings.
- **33596 (Buckhorn, River Hills, Diamond Hill):** Median sold price $462,000 to $472,000. The Newsome High School zone premium adds $20,000 to $35,000 per square foot compared to comparable 33594 product.

**Apollo Beach** (ZIP 33572) is tracking a typical home value of approximately $439,000 to $504,000 as of mid-2026, depending on the source, with days on market running 52 to 90 days and 300 to 400 active listings. That wide range reflects the difference between inland non-waterfront homes (which compete directly with Valrico on price) and canal-front or Tampa Bay access homes (which carry a $100,000 to $300,000 waterfront premium).

For non-waterfront homes, the two markets are remarkably close in price. A buyer with a $450,000 budget can find a 3 to 4 bedroom single-family home in either market. The waterfront premium in Apollo Beach is what pushes many listings above $500,000.

## What You Get for the Money

**In Valrico at $420,000 to $470,000**, a buyer in the 33596 ZIP code typically finds:
- 2,000 to 2,800 square feet
- 4 bedrooms, 2 to 3 bathrooms
- Established neighborhood with mature trees, 20 to 30-year-old homes
- Lot size of 0.15 to 0.25 acres
- Poured concrete block construction standard throughout the area
- Newsome High School zone access in most of 33596
- Access to community amenities depending on the subdivision (pool, tennis, playground)

**In Apollo Beach at $420,000 to $470,000**, a buyer typically finds:
- 2,000 to 2,600 square feet in the Waterset master-planned community
- Newer construction (2015 to 2026 homes dominate Waterset's newer phases)
- HOA and CDD fees: Waterset carries CDD assessments of approximately $1,200 to $2,000 annually on top of HOA fees
- Inland lots of 0.12 to 0.18 acres, smaller than Valrico's established neighborhoods
- More uniform newer construction with similar finishes across a development
- No Tampa Bay water access at this price point in a non-waterfront community

The core tradeoff is **age and character versus newness**. Valrico gives you established trees, larger lots, and neighborhoods that have been fully built out for decades. Apollo Beach's newer master-planned areas like Waterset offer newer homes, amenity-rich communities, and contemporary floor plans, but on smaller lots with CDD carrying costs.

## Schools: Where the Real Difference Lies

For families, school quality often drives the final decision in this comparison.

**Valrico 33596:**
- Buckhorn Elementary and Cimino Elementary (both high-rated)
- Burns Middle School and Rodgers Middle School
- **Newsome High School** (consistently rated A by the Florida Department of Education, top 10 in Hillsborough County)

The Newsome HS zone is the single most durable price-support factor in all of east Hillsborough County. Buyers pay a premium to be in Newsome zone, and that premium holds up even in soft markets because demand from families does not disappear.

**Apollo Beach 33572:**
- Doby Elementary (well-rated, B/B+)
- Eisenhower Middle School
- **East Bay High School** (solid, B-rated, good extracurriculars)
- **Waterset Charter School** (K-8, located inside the community)
- SouthShore Montessori at Waterset (PK-K option)

East Bay High is a good school with strong programs, but it does not carry the market premium that Newsome does. The Waterset Charter School is a genuine differentiator for K-8 families who prefer a charter option within walking distance of home.

If your highest priority is high school quality and its impact on resale value, Valrico 33596 wins this category. If you have younger children and value the charter school option inside a master-planned community, Apollo Beach has a compelling answer.

## Commute to Tampa

Both communities require a commute, but the routing is different.

**From Apollo Beach:** Most residents take US-41 north to I-75, then I-75 to their Tampa destination. The community entrance is approximately 0.9 miles from I-75. Downtown Tampa is roughly 16 miles from Waterset. Expect 25 to 40 minutes in normal traffic, and 45 to 60 minutes in peak morning commute conditions heading north on I-75.

**From Valrico:** SR-60 or Causeway Boulevard takes you west to I-75 (from 33596) or Brandon's Veterans Expressway/Selmon Expressway connections. Downtown Tampa is approximately 20 to 25 miles from central Valrico. Expect 30 to 45 minutes in normal conditions, and 50 to 70 minutes during peak I-75 traffic.

Both corridors face the same I-75 northbound congestion challenge in the morning. Apollo Beach has a slight raw mileage advantage, though Valrico buyers closer to SR-60 can access the Selmon Expressway, which provides a faster route to Ybor City, South Tampa, and downtown compared to I-75.

If you work in the SouthShore employment corridor along US-301 or in the Amazon, FedEx, and logistics hub zones near I-75 and SR-674, Apollo Beach is the stronger choice for commute. If you work in central or north Tampa, the difference between the two communities is marginal.

## Flood Insurance and Insurance Costs

This is a significant differentiator that buyers sometimes overlook until they receive their insurance quote.

**Valrico:** Most of the established residential neighborhoods in 33594 and 33596 are located in FEMA Flood Zone X, which carries minimal flood risk and does not require mandatory flood insurance. Standard HO-3 homeowner's insurance in Valrico runs approximately $2,800 to $4,200 annually for a $400,000 to $470,000 home, depending on roof age, construction, and carrier. Without mandatory flood coverage, the total annual insurance burden is manageable.

**Apollo Beach:** Properties with canal access or Tampa Bay frontage sit in Flood Zone AE or VE, which requires mandatory flood insurance. NFIP (National Flood Insurance Program) premiums for waterfront properties run $1,500 to $4,500 or more annually depending on the structure's elevation certificate and flood zone. Even non-waterfront homes in Apollo Beach closer to tidal wetlands may carry Zone X (shaded) designations with higher voluntary flood insurance costs than inland Valrico.

For buyers specifically targeting a waterfront home in Apollo Beach, budget $4,500 to $8,000 annually in combined homeowner's and flood insurance, compared to $2,800 to $4,200 for a Valrico non-waterfront home. That gap runs $150 to $300 more per month.

## HOA and CDD Fees

**Valrico:** Many established neighborhoods in Valrico have no HOA or carry modest fees of $50 to $150 per month. River Hills Country Club carries a mandatory HOA in the $700 to $1,200 per year range. Diamond Hill has a low HOA. No CDD fees in most established Valrico communities. Newer sections near Crestwood Estates or Northwood Estates do carry CDDs.

**Apollo Beach (Waterset):** The Waterset master-planned community carries HOA fees plus CDD assessments. CDD assessments at Waterset run approximately $1,200 to $2,000 annually and appear as a line item on your property tax bill, not a separate check. Combined with the HOA, the monthly carrying cost adds $200 to $350 to your effective housing payment versus many Valrico neighborhoods. Older Apollo Beach neighborhoods outside of Waterset may have no HOA or low HOA fees.

## Market Conditions in Q4 2026

Both communities are tracking buyer-favorable conditions entering Q4 2026.

**Valrico:** 240 to 260 active listings in Valrico proper, 50 to 68 average days on market, 37 to 42 percent of listings showing price reductions, 96 to 97 percent sale-to-list ratio. Sellers are offering concessions including closing cost credits and 2-1 mortgage rate buydowns. The rate lock effect is constraining new listings: homeowners with sub-4% mortgages are holding, limiting fresh supply.

**Apollo Beach:** 300 to 400 active listings across the community, 52 to 90 days on market. The expanded inventory includes both Waterset new construction and resale across the broader 33572 ZIP. Buyer leverage is real, particularly for resale listings that have been on the market 60 or more days.

Both markets favor patient, pre-approved buyers who are not competing in multiple offer situations. Sellers who are not motivated to close are sitting, while motivated sellers are negotiating meaningfully on price, terms, or concessions.

## 10-Year Resale Outlook

The Newsome High School zone creates a price floor in Valrico 33596 that is hard to replicate. As long as Newsome maintains its reputation, families will continue to target that zone, providing durable demand that sustains resale values across market cycles. Valrico 33594 (Bloomingdale area) benefits from the same county-wide appreciation dynamics as the broader east Hillsborough market.

Apollo Beach's resale value is tied more heavily to the broader SouthShore growth narrative: the continued expansion of the US-41/US-301 employment corridor, the eventual completion of infrastructure improvements, and the community's appeal to water-lifestyle buyers. Waterset in particular has the density and amenity package to hold value well compared to standalone older Apollo Beach subdivisions.

Both markets have sound long-term fundamentals. Valrico has the more established track record. Apollo Beach has higher upside potential if the SouthShore employment corridor continues to expand, offset by higher insurance and CDD carrying costs.

## Which Buyer Fits Each Market

**Choose Valrico 33596 if:**
- School zone is your top priority, specifically Newsome High School
- You want a larger established lot with mature trees
- You prefer lower monthly carrying costs (no CDD, lower insurance)
- You want an established neighborhood with a proven resale track record
- Your budget is $400,000 to $500,000 for a non-new construction home

**Choose Valrico 33594 if:**
- Your budget is $340,000 to $400,000
- You want the Bloomingdale area established character at a more accessible price
- Lower property taxes and insurance are a priority

**Choose Apollo Beach (non-waterfront) if:**
- Newer construction and contemporary floor plans matter more than lot size
- You want a master-planned amenity community (Waterset's pools, trails, fitness center)
- Your children are K-8 age and the Waterset Charter School appeals to you
- You work in the SouthShore or south Tampa area
- You are comfortable with CDD assessments as a long-term carrying cost

**Choose Apollo Beach (waterfront) if:**
- Water access and the coastal lifestyle are non-negotiable
- Your budget exceeds $550,000 to $600,000
- You have factored in full flood insurance costs
- You are buying for lifestyle, not strictly investment optimization
