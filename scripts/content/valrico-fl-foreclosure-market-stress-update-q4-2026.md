## Hillsborough County Foreclosure Filings: Q2 and Q3 2026 Data

The most reliable source for foreclosure filing trends in Hillsborough County is the University of Florida Shimberg Center for Housing Studies, which tracks monthly filing counts from the county clerk's court records. Here is what the 2026 data shows through the most recent available reporting period.

Monthly foreclosure filings in Hillsborough County through H1 2026:

- January 2026: 277 filings
- February 2026: 247 filings
- March 2026: 327 filings
- April 2026: 340 filings
- May 2026: 324 filings
- June 2026: 279 filings

For comparison, November 2025 produced 243 filings. The year-over-year trend through mid-2026 shows filings running roughly 15 to 35 percent above the same months in 2025, which itself was above 2024. The Florida foreclosure help blog covering Tampa Bay activity attributes the increase to expiring forbearance agreements, rising insurance costs, increasing property tax assessments, and overall affordability pressure.

That said, the absolute numbers require context. Hillsborough County has approximately 550,000 housing units. Monthly foreclosure filings in the 250 to 340 range represent roughly 0.05 percent of the housing stock per month. The foreclosure activity that defined 2008 to 2012 saw Hillsborough County recording thousands of filings per month, not hundreds. The current pace of filings is elevated compared to the 2020 to 2024 period of near-historic lows, but it is not elevated compared to any historical norm outside of that unusual low-distress period.

## What Q3 2026 Showed in Valrico

Zooming from county-level to Valrico specifically, the picture is consistent with the county trend: foreclosure activity has increased from historically low levels but remains well below any threshold that would materially change buyer or seller strategy.

The mid-year analysis found approximately 183 active foreclosure filings across Hillsborough County in June 2026. By September and October, that count had grown modestly as new filings added to the pipeline faster than cases resolved. Industry data sources tracking active Redfin foreclosure listings showed Hillsborough County active foreclosure and pre-foreclosure inventory running in the 180 to 220 range through late Q3 2026, still a thin market in a county of 550,000 housing units.

Within the Valrico submarkets specifically, the 33594 and 33596 ZIP codes have higher median household incomes and lower leverage ratios than much of Hillsborough County. The buyers who purchased in Valrico during 2020 to 2022 mostly had conventional loans with 20 percent down or more, 15-year equity build periods, or significant equity from prior-home proceeds. That ownership profile is more resilient to rate-induced stress than the high-LTV buyer cohort that drove the 2006 to 2008 wave.

The neighborhoods with the highest vulnerability to foreclosure stress in Hillsborough County are concentrated in areas with high FHA and VA loan concentrations, buyers who stretched to their maximum approval amounts in 2021 and 2022 on minimal down payments, and areas where insurance cost increases have pushed total monthly payments into unaffordable territory. That profile describes parts of eastern Hillsborough at lower price points and some of the master-planned communities near Wimauma and southern Riverview more than it describes established Valrico.

## Distress Indicators in the Active Valrico Listing Market

Foreclosure filings are a lagging indicator. The leading indicators of market stress are visible in the active listing data right now.

**Price reduction rate:** Approximately 35 to 42 percent of active listings across Valrico carried a price reduction as of early November 2026. That is elevated compared to 2021 and 2022, when price reductions were rare, but it is consistent with a market that has normalized back toward historical patterns. In a healthy balanced market, a 20 to 30 percent price reduction rate is typical. The current 35 to 42 percent rate indicates sellers who listed at peak-season optimism are now adjusting to current buyer demand. This is not a distress signal. It is a correction in seller expectations.

**Days on market:** The average days on market across Valrico runs approximately 45 to 68 days depending on price tier and ZIP as of November 2026. In 33596, well-priced Newsome-zone homes go under contract in 21 to 35 days. The extended marketing times are a function of elevated inventory and mortgage rate pressure on buyer purchasing power, not of distressed seller circumstances in most cases.

**Active inventory:** Valrico's active listing count reached approximately 240 to 260 homes in late October and has stabilized at that level entering November. That is about 43 percent higher than the 172 listings counted in August 2026. The inventory expansion reflects normal seasonal patterns, some move-up sellers who listed for fall before realizing they did not have to rush, and a small number of sellers exiting investment properties as carrying costs have increased.

**Sale-to-list ratio:** The blended Valrico sale-to-list ratio is running approximately 96.5 to 97.5 percent, meaning buyers are closing at 2.5 to 3.5 percent below original asking prices on average. Homes that have been listed 60 days or more and taken a price reduction are closing at 94 to 96 percent of original list price. That is leverage, but it is negotiating leverage, not foreclosure-level distress.

## The Insurance Cost Stress Factor

The most underappreciated source of financial stress in the Hillsborough County housing market is not mortgage rate increases but insurance cost increases. Florida homeowners insurance premiums have risen sharply since 2022, with Valrico homeowners facing typical HO-3 renewal rates of $2,800 to $4,200 per year on homes in the $380,000 to $470,000 range. That is approximately 30 to 50 percent higher than 2020 premium levels for comparable coverage.

For a homeowner who purchased in 2021 at a $380,000 price with a 3.0 percent mortgage, the original insurance estimate at closing may have been $1,800 to $2,200 per year. Renewal premiums at $3,500 to $4,000 per year represent an additional $1,300 to $1,800 per year in monthly carrying costs. Combined with property tax increases from reassessment cycles, the total increase in non-mortgage monthly costs for a 2021 buyer can reach $200 to $300 per month above their original underwriting.

This is the scenario creating the most financial pressure for homeowners who stretched to their limits in 2021 and 2022. It is not a catastrophic default trigger for most Valrico households, but it is contributing to decisions to sell and right-size rather than carry a home with rising costs.

## Are Foreclosure Bargains Coming to Valrico?

This is the question at the heart of many buyer conversations. The answer is: not in any volume that would materially reshape the market.

The foreclosure pipeline in Hillsborough County has extended timelines. Florida is a judicial foreclosure state, meaning every foreclosure must pass through the court system. From initial filing to auction can take 12 to 24 months or longer, depending on case complexity, negotiated workouts, and court scheduling. Filings that entered the pipeline in mid-2026 are unlikely to reach auction before late 2027 or 2028 at the earliest.

The more productive opportunity for value-focused buyers right now is not the foreclosure market but the motivated-seller resale market. Listings that have been active for 60 days or more and have taken at least one price reduction represent sellers who are aligned with current buyer reality. Those sellers are willing to negotiate on price, closing costs, and repairs in ways that spring season sellers were not.

Buyers who want to position for a Valrico purchase in this window should focus on:

- Listings above 60 days on market in their target ZIP and price tier
- Sellers who have already reduced price at least once
- Properties where the gap between current list price and comparable closed sales is 3 to 5 percent or more
- Sellers who have already vacated the property, which typically increases motivation

Pre-foreclosure listings, where sellers are behind on payments but have not yet reached auction, can also offer genuine value. The [Valrico pre-foreclosure page](/valrico-pre-foreclosure/) covers what that process looks like from a buyer's perspective.

## Recession Indicators: Updated Q4 2026 Read

Looking beyond the local foreclosure data to broader economic signals, the picture as of November 2026 is mixed.

The U.S. labor market has remained resilient through 2026. Hillsborough County unemployment is running near 3.6 to 3.9 percent, which is healthy by historical standards. Tampa Bay's economic diversification across healthcare, logistics, finance, and defense spending at MacDill AFB provides a buffer against single-sector downturns.

The mortgage delinquency rate nationally remains well below the 2008 to 2012 levels that produced a true foreclosure wave. Serious delinquencies, defined as 90 or more days past due, are running at approximately 1.5 to 1.8 percent of outstanding mortgages, compared to peaks above 8 percent in 2010.

The risks that bear watching through Q4 2026 and into 2027 are:

- Mortgage rate persistence above 6.5 percent extending buyer affordability pressure into 2027
- Insurance market conditions in Florida that force additional rate increases on homeowners
- Any softening in the Tampa Bay employment base that reduces household income and increases delinquency rates
- A continuation of the inventory expansion trend, which could shift negotiating leverage further toward buyers

None of these signals, individually or collectively, point toward the kind of market dislocation that produces the foreclosure volumes Hillsborough County saw in 2009 and 2010. The equity cushion that most Valrico homeowners carry from the 2020 to 2022 appreciation cycle provides a significant buffer.
