## Days on Market: October Tells a Clear Story

Days on market in October 2026 reflect Valrico's well-established two-tier pattern.

Correctly priced, move-in-ready homes in the core Bloomingdale section of 33596, especially those zoned for Newsome High School, are going under contract in 18 to 32 days. Homes in this category at $420,000 to $480,000 are seeing two to three serious showings per week.

Homes in 33594 priced between $350,000 and $420,000 are taking an average of 42 to 58 days. The wide range reflects condition and pricing discipline more than location.

Listings that were overpriced in the spring and summer and have taken multiple price reductions are carrying 90 to 130 days on market and often still trading below their first list price. If your home has been sitting, the problem is almost certainly price, not the market.

**Across Hillsborough County:** The overall market average is approximately 45 to 55 days on market, with the county as a whole carrying approximately 4.3 months of supply as of early October, per recent market reports. That is solidly balanced to slightly buyer-favorable territory at the county level. Valrico's 33596, at approximately 2.2 to 2.5 months of supply, is the outlier, consistently tighter than the surrounding area due to school-zone demand.

## Inventory: October Dynamics

Active inventory in Valrico is running approximately 155 to 175 homes across both ZIPs in October. That is slightly lower than the peak of approximately 185 to 200 homes seen at the end of summer, which is typical for this time of year.

The seasonal pullback in inventory entering October is not because demand surged. It is because some sellers who did not sell in the summer made a rational decision to withdraw and wait for the spring season rather than carry a home through winter. This withdrawal tightens supply modestly and gives remaining listings slightly less competition.

For buyers, the October market offers a genuine opportunity: motivated sellers who listed in the spring and summer are now into their third or fourth month with carrying costs mounting. These sellers tend to be more negotiable on price, closing costs, and repair credits than they were in March. The fall negotiating window is real.

## The November 3 Variable: Florida's Property Tax Amendment

The most significant non-rate event in Valrico's market calendar before year-end is the November 3, 2026 ballot. Florida Amendment 3 proposes to expand the non-school homestead exemption from $25,000 to $150,000 for the 2027 tax year and $250,000 for the 2028 tax year.

For a Valrico homeowner with a property assessed at $413,000 with a standard $50,000 homestead exemption today:
- Current non-school taxable value: approximately $363,000
- With Amendment 3's $150,000 non-school exemption: non-school taxable value drops to approximately $263,000
- Estimated annual savings: approximately $850 to $950 per year depending on the applicable millage rate
- Monthly equivalent: approximately $70 to $80 per month

If the amendment passes with the required 60% approval, every homesteaded Valrico buyer who closes before December 31, 2026 and files homestead exemption by March 1, 2027 captures this benefit beginning on their first full tax year. Buyers who close in 2027 also benefit, of course, but the point is there is no buyer-side penalty to acting in fall 2026 relative to spring 2027 from a tax perspective. Sellers benefit too: the amendment makes Valrico homes more affordable to own, which supports values.

## October Price Reduction Activity

Approximately 34 to 38% of active Valrico listings have taken at least one price reduction as of early October. That is a meaningful figure but not alarming by historical standards. For context, during 2021 and 2022, fewer than 5% of listings reduced price because demand absorbed everything. The current reduction rate reflects a market returning to normal, not a market in distress.

What the data actually shows:
- **Price reductions are concentrated in 33594 above $430,000.** Sellers who listed at spring-market prices and have not adjusted are sitting. Below $400,000 in 33594, inventory is tighter and reductions are less common.
- **33596 reductions are rare below $500,000.** The Newsome zone keeps demand tight. Reductions in 33596 are mostly on outlier listings priced above the $550,000 to $600,000 range.
- **Condition matters more than reductions.** A home that reduced price but needs a new roof, outdated kitchen, or deferred maintenance will still sit. A well-prepared home at asking price moves faster than a reduced-price home that needs work.

## What Sellers Need to Do Differently in October 2026

October is a legitimate selling window but it requires a different approach than spring.

**Price to today, not to spring.** The spring 2026 pricing ceiling was the market's high point this year. October buyers have access to all of summer's closed comps and they know exactly what comparable homes have sold for in the past 60 to 90 days. A seller pricing based on February or March 2026 expectations will be corrected by the market within 45 days.

**Pre-listing condition matters most in fall.** Fall buyers are making decisions they plan to close on before the holidays. They do not want projects. A move-in-ready home in October competes against a smaller pool of listings than it did in May, but that pool is also pickier because fall buyers are serious.

**Seller concessions are expected.** Contributing 2 to 3% toward buyer closing costs ($8,200 to $12,450 on a $413,000 home) or offering a mortgage rate buydown is now standard practice in Valrico for homes above $380,000. This is not a sign of weakness, it is what the current market requires. ## What Buyers Should Know in October 2026

October is underrated as a buying window. Here is why:

**Motivated sellers are concentrated.** The homes that have not sold since spring, summer, and early fall are now carrying months of holding costs. Sellers who have not reduced to market value and who have not accepted the seasonal reality are often more flexible in private negotiation than their list price suggests.

**Less competition.** October buyer traffic is approximately 30 to 40% lower than April and May in Valrico. You can take your time. You can visit the same house twice. You can negotiate without a competing offer showing up the same day.

**School-year visibility.** Families who need to be settled before the school year have already closed. October buyers in the Newsome zone have the full enrollment picture for the current year, not a guess from the spring.

**Inspection contingencies are fully restored.** The 2021 and 2022 practice of waiving inspections to compete is gone. Every offer should include a standard 10 to 15-day inspection period, and sellers are accepting it.

## Q4 2026 Outlook: What to Expect Through December

The Valrico market will follow its historical seasonal pattern for the remainder of 2026.

**October through mid-November:** The most active fall window. Buyers motivated to close before the holidays are in the market. This is when sellers who price correctly have the best shot at a pre-holiday close.

**Mid-November through December:** Activity drops sharply. The Thanksgiving and holiday period produces the fewest showings of the year. Homes that do not go under contract before Thanksgiving will almost certainly carry into 2027.

**Price movement:** Minimal price change in either direction is expected through year-end. The Valrico market is not set up for a significant correction, with 33596 still under 3 months of supply, nor for meaningful appreciation given flat year-over-year trends in 33594. The November 3 amendment vote is the most likely catalyst for any sentiment shift.

**Rate outlook:** No consensus among forecasters points to a rate cut significant enough to change the October market before year-end. Buyers waiting for 5.5% rates before acting are almost certainly waiting until 2027.
