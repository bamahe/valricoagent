## Hillsborough County FL Real Estate Market: 2026 Year-End Report for Buyers and Sellers

Hillsborough County is one of the most dynamic real estate markets in Florida. With Tampa as the economic anchor, suburban growth corridors in Riverview, Wesley Chapel, and Brandon, and established communities like Valrico holding steady value, the county covers every price point and buyer profile. Here is the full picture as we close out 2026.

## Hillsborough County Housing Market by the Numbers: Q4 2026

Based on Momentum Realty's October 2026 data sourced from Stellar MLS:

- **Median sold price:** approximately $399,900, up 0.6 percent year over year
- **Active listings:** approximately 6,574 homes countywide
- **Months of supply:** approximately 4.0 months (balanced market definition: 4 to 6 months)
- **Median days to contract:** approximately 42 days
- **Price reductions:** 26 to 30 percent of active listings have taken at least one price reduction

Zillow's October 2026 average home value figure for the county is slightly lower at $375,501, down 0.9 percent year over year. The gap between Zillow (a valuation model) and MLS closed-sale data reflects methodology differences, not a data error. MLS sold prices are more reliable for buyers and sellers making actual decisions.

The key read: Hillsborough County is a balanced market. Neither buyers nor sellers have a commanding advantage countywide, but conditions vary significantly by submarket.

## The Insurance Factor: Still Suppressing Demand in 2026

Florida homeowner's insurance continues to be the single largest cost factor beyond the mortgage that shapes Hillsborough County demand. Average annual homeowners insurance premiums in Tampa Bay run approximately $3,815 per year according to published market data for 2026. That is $318 per month added to a buyer's payment before escrow.

For a buyer purchasing a $400,000 home with 20 percent down at current rates, insurance adds meaningfully to the monthly payment, pushing total housing cost higher than the principal and interest payment alone suggests. This is a primary reason why some buyers are looking at higher-priced but newer-construction homes in Riverview and Wesley Chapel, where newer roofs typically generate lower insurance premiums.

**What buyers can do:**

- Get insurance quotes before making an offer. Roof age over 15 years often triggers non-renewal or significant premium increases.
- Ask sellers for a roof age disclosure upfront. A 20-plus-year-old roof is a negotiating point.
- Look at carrier availability in the specific area, not just the county average. Some parts of Hillsborough near floodplain or coastal zones have significantly higher insurance costs.
- Consider wind mitigation inspections post-closing. Credits can reduce premiums by hundreds of dollars annually on qualifying homes.

## Amendment 3: What It Means for 2027 Property Tax Bills

Florida Amendment 3, passed in the November 2026 election, expands the homestead exemption, increasing the Save Our Homes portability cap and reducing assessed value growth for primary residences. For Hillsborough County homeowners, the practical effect in 2027 and beyond is lower assessed value growth on homestead-exempt properties, which translates to smaller annual property tax increases.

For buyers: this makes buying a primary residence in Hillsborough County in late 2026 or early 2027 advantageous from a property tax lock-in perspective. Locking in your assessed value under Amendment 3's expanded framework before prices rise further is a legitimate long-term tax strategy.

For sellers: existing homeowners with significant Save Our Homes accumulations now have increased portability benefits, which makes trading up or trading down within the county more financially attractive than it was before Amendment 3.

## Year-End Buyer Strategy for Hillsborough County

December is historically the softest month for Hillsborough County real estate transactions. Fewer buyers are active, listings that have been on the market since summer are at their most motivated, and sellers are often willing to accept terms that would not have worked in spring or fall.

**Practical December 2026 buyer tactics:**

**Target 60-plus day listings.** In any Hillsborough County zip code, homes with 60 or more days on market have sellers who have already mentally adjusted to the new reality. Use that leverage to ask for seller-paid closing costs, rate buydowns, or price reductions that bring the home to current market value.

**Builder incentives peak at year-end.** Large builders like D.R. Horton, Pulte, and Lennar have Q4 close goals. December and January are historically the best months to negotiate rate buydowns, closing cost credits, and design upgrades on new construction. This applies in Riverview, Plant City, Wimauma, and Wesley Chapel communities specifically.

**Lock your rate.** The Federal Reserve's rate path in early 2027 remains uncertain. If you are under contract and your lender allows it, locking before year-end removes one risk variable from the transaction.

**Get pre-approved, not just pre-qualified.** December inventory is thin and competitive in the best submarkets (Valrico 33596, FishHawk, parts of Wesley Chapel). A fully underwritten pre-approval competes better than a soft pre-qualification letter.

## Hillsborough County Market Forecast: What to Expect in 2027

Based on current inventory, rate environment, and demand drivers:

**Prices:** Expect flat to 2 percent appreciation countywide in 2027. The outsized gains of 2021 to 2023 are behind us. The bigger variable is insurance cost reform. If Florida's regulatory environment continues to improve and carriers increase market participation, reduced insurance costs could unlock demand that is currently suppressed, supporting a modest price increase.

**Inventory:** Likely to remain elevated through early 2027 as new construction completions from 2025 and 2026 pipeline continue to deliver. The balance tips back toward sellers in mid-2027 if demand holds.

**Rate environment:** Current consensus among economists leans toward modest rate decreases through 2027, but nothing dramatic. Buyers waiting for a return to 3 or 4 percent rates are almost certainly waiting for a scenario that does not materialize. The buyers locking rates today in the mid-to-high 6s are likely to refinance within two to three years if the consensus holds.

**Valrico specifically:** The Newsome HS zone premium is structural, not cyclical. As long as Newsome High School maintains its top-tier rating and Hillsborough County maintains its in-migration trend, Valrico 33596 will hold value better than most comparable county submarkets.

## Frequently Asked Questions: Hillsborough County Real Estate 2026

**Is it a good time to buy a home in Hillsborough County in late 2026?**
For buyers with stable income and a plan to stay at least 3 to 5 years, yes. Prices are flat to slightly down from peak, seller concessions are common, and Amendment 3 locks in a favorable assessed value baseline going into 2027. The main risk is buying overpriced relative to current comps, which is preventable with solid agent representation and market data.

**What is the median home price in Hillsborough County in 2026?**
MLS closed sale data puts the countywide median at approximately $399,900 as of October 2026, up 0.6 percent year over year. This blends everything from $280,000 condos in Sun City Center to $600,000-plus homes in FishHawk Ranch and New Tampa. Specific area medians vary significantly.

**Which Hillsborough County neighborhoods are holding value best?**
Valrico 33596 (Newsome HS zone), FishHawk Ranch (Lithia/33547), and select parts of New Tampa (33647) have shown the strongest price retention in 2026. These areas combine strong school zones with desirable community character and limited new construction competition nearby.

**How much is homeowners insurance in Hillsborough County?**
Averages run approximately $3,815 per year for a typical single-family home, but premiums vary significantly by roof age, construction type, proximity to water, and carrier. Homes with roofs under 10 years old with hip-roof construction can often find rates meaningfully below average. Homes with aging flat roofs or in higher-risk flood zones can pay significantly more.
