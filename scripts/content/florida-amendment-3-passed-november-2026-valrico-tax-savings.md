## What Amendment 3 Actually Does

Amendment 3 expands the non-school homestead property tax exemption in two stages:

- **January 1, 2027:** The non-school homestead exemption increases from $50,000 to $150,000.
- **January 1, 2028:** The exemption increases further to $250,000, with inflation adjustments beginning in 2029.

The amendment also reduces the annual assessment growth cap for non-homestead properties from 10 percent to 5 percent, which affects investment properties, commercial real estate, and second homes.

There are two things Amendment 3 does NOT change. First, school district property taxes are unaffected. The school millage applies separately, and the new exemption does not reduce your school tax portion. Second, your 2026 property tax bill is unaffected. The changes take effect for the 2027 tax year. Your November 2026 Hillsborough County bill reflects current law, not the new amendment.

## The Dollar Math for Valrico Homeowners

The savings depend on your assessed value and Hillsborough County's combined non-school millage rate. Here is how the numbers work at common Valrico price points.

Hillsborough County's combined non-school millage rate is approximately 11.0 to 11.5 mills for most unincorporated Valrico properties, covering county government, fire, library, and other non-school levies.

**Under current law (before Amendment 3):**
A $413,000 assessed value with the existing $50,000 homestead exemption yields a non-school taxable value of $363,000. At 11.0 mills, that produces approximately $3,993 in non-school taxes per year.

**Under Amendment 3 starting in 2027:**
The same $413,000 assessed value with the new $150,000 non-school exemption yields a non-school taxable value of $263,000. At 11.0 mills, that produces approximately $2,893 in non-school taxes per year. That is a savings of approximately $1,100 per year starting in 2027.

By 2028, when the exemption reaches $250,000, the non-school taxable value on a $413,000 assessed home falls to $163,000, producing approximately $1,793 per year. The total annual savings at full implementation is approximately $2,200 per year, or about $183 per month.

Hillsborough County Property Appraiser Bob Henriquez estimated that the average Hillsborough homeowner would save approximately $2,000 per year once the amendment is fully in place, which aligns with the math above. See the [Hillsborough County Tax Collector property tax reform page](https://www.hillstaxfl.gov/property-tax-reform/) for the county's official guidance on the change.

If you are a buyer who has been waiting on the sidelines in Valrico, the passage of Amendment 3 adds another reason to act in November or December 2026 rather than waiting for spring.

Here is why timing matters. To receive the new homestead exemption for the 2027 tax year, you must qualify for homestead exemption by January 1, 2027. That means closing on your Valrico home before December 31, 2026 and filing your homestead exemption application with the Hillsborough County Property Appraiser by March 1, 2027.

Buyers who close in January or February 2027 instead of December 2026 miss the first year of Amendment 3 savings entirely. For a home in the $420,000 to $450,000 range, that first year of savings is worth approximately $1,100 to $1,200 in reduced tax costs.

## The Non-Homestead Assessment Cap Change

Amendment 3 also reduces the annual assessment cap on non-homestead properties from 10 percent per year to 5 percent per year. This affects:

- Rental properties and investment properties in Valrico
- Vacation homes and second homes
- Commercial real estate

For landlords who own rental properties in Valrico, the 5 percent cap reduces how fast your assessed value can rise in hot market years. During the 2020 to 2022 appreciation cycle, assessed values on non-homestead properties increased sharply because the existing 10 percent cap allowed significant catch-up. The new 5 percent cap will moderate that dynamic in future appreciation cycles. It does not reduce your current assessed value or your current tax bill.

## What Amendment 3 Does Not Do

Several misconceptions about Amendment 3 circulated before the vote. Here is the accurate picture.

**Amendment 3 does not eliminate property taxes.** You still pay property taxes on the assessed value above the exemption threshold. For a $413,000 assessed value, you still have a taxable value of $163,000 for non-school purposes at full implementation, and your school taxes remain calculated at the full assessed value minus the original $25,000 school exemption.

**Amendment 3 does not apply to your 2026 bill.** The amendment takes effect for the 2027 tax year. Your November 2026 Hillsborough County bill reflects current law.

**Amendment 3 does not change the Save Our Homes assessment cap.** The 3 percent annual assessment cap on homesteaded properties remains unchanged. The Save Our Homes portability rules, which allow you to transfer your accumulated SOH differential to a new Florida home within three years, also remain unchanged. For a full explanation of how portability works, see the [Florida Save Our Homes portability guide for Valrico sellers](/blog/florida-save-our-homes-portability-valrico-sellers-guide/).

**Amendment 3 does not affect non-homestead owners' current bills.** The reduced non-homestead assessment cap applies in future years when assessed values are rising. It does not create a refund or reduction in your current tax bill.

## Revenue Impact and What It Means Locally

Amendment 3's savings for homeowners come at a cost to local government revenue. The Florida Policy Institute projected the amendment would reduce Hillsborough County revenue by approximately $971 million over the first two fiscal years following implementation. The Hillsborough County Property Appraiser's office estimated the county could lose approximately $300 million annually in non-school tax revenue.

That revenue reduction will force local government decisions about service levels, capital projects, and potentially millage rate adjustments. It does not change your 2026 or 2027 tax bills, but it is a factor in the long-term fiscal picture for Hillsborough County services.

## Action Items for Valrico Homeowners

**If you are a current homeowner:** No action is required for 2027. The Hillsborough County Property Appraiser will apply the new exemption automatically for homeowners who are already homesteaded. You do not need to refile for homestead exemption.

**If you are a buyer closing before December 31, 2026:** File your homestead exemption application with the Hillsborough County Property Appraiser by March 1, 2027 to qualify for the Amendment 3 benefit in the 2027 tax year. The online filing portal is available at the Property Appraiser's website.

**If you are a buyer planning to close in 2027:** You will receive the Amendment 3 benefit for the 2027 tax year as long as you establish homestead exemption by January 1, 2027. If you close after January 1, 2027, your first Amendment 3 benefit begins with the 2028 tax year.

**If you own rental or investment property in Valrico:** No action is required. The reduced non-homestead assessment cap applies automatically in future years when assessed values are rising. It does not reduce your current assessed value.
