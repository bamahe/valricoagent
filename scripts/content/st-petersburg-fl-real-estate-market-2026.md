## St. Petersburg FL Real Estate Market Overview 2026

The St. Petersburg FL real estate market in 2026 is in a broad cooling trend, though that statement masks significant variation by ZIP code, property type, and neighborhood. [Zillow's Home Value Index](https://www.zillow.com/st-petersburg-fl/home-values/) places the typical St. Pete home value at approximately $355,759 as of June 2026, down 3.9% year over year. A separate Zillow snapshot pegged the figure at $354,241, down 4.8%. Either way, the direction is the same: modest price softening after several years of pandemic-era appreciation.

The Q3 2026 median sale price, as tracked by local market reporting, sits in the $400,000 to $408,000 range for single-family homes citywide. That gap between the Zillow home value index (which includes all property types and uses a smoothed algorithm) and the median sale price reflects the mix of condos and villas dragging the overall index lower. Single-family detached homes in desirable St. Pete neighborhoods are holding value better than the condo and attached-home segment.

Inventory has risen materially across Pinellas County in 2026. The condominium segment in particular has seen a surge of listings tied to Florida's new structural reserve funding requirements for buildings three stories and taller, passed in the wake of the 2021 Surfside collapse. Sellers who delayed compliance decisions are now listing. That dynamic is specific to St. Pete's condo-heavy waterfront corridors and matters less in its single-family suburban neighborhoods.

## ZIP Code Breakdown: St. Petersburg by the Numbers

St. Petersburg spans multiple ZIP codes with meaningfully different price points, buyer demographics, and market velocity. Here is the current picture.

### 33702 (Northeast St. Pete)

The 33702 ZIP covers the northeastern section of the city, including areas north of 62nd Avenue North. This is one of the most buyer-favorable ZIPs in the city in 2026. The median sale price is approximately $319,000. Homes are averaging 55 days on market before going under contract. Months of supply sits at approximately 5.5, which puts buyers firmly in the driver's seat for negotiations. For buyers whose budget tops out around $300,000 to $340,000 and who want to be inside St. Pete city limits, 33702 is where the best deals are in 2026.

### 33703 and 33704 (Central and North St. Pete)

The 33703 ZIP covers central and north St. Pete, including portions of the Coffee Pot Bayou waterfront area where canal homes and bay access properties command a premium. The median in 33703 sits at approximately $415,000. Days on market is approximately 46 and months of supply is approximately 4.4, making this a balanced market. Buyers have negotiating room but not the seller-softening leverage visible in 33702.

The 33704 ZIP is the premium tier. This ZIP covers the Old Northeast historic district, Venetian Isles, and the neighborhoods directly north of downtown. The median sale price in 33704 is approximately $758,000, though that figure rests on a relatively small sales pool and should be treated as directional rather than definitive. What is clear is that 33704 is the tightest market in the city. Months of supply drops to approximately 1.9, which is seller-leaning even by current standards. Buyers shopping for historic bungalows with original hardwood floors, proximity to downtown walkability, and access to the waterfront park system are competing in a different market than the rest of the city.

Historic Kenwood, just west of downtown, is notable for its Craftsman bungalow stock and arts-focused residential character. Home prices in the Kenwood area run below Old Northeast but above the city median, and the neighborhood has drawn significant renovation investment over the past decade.

### 33712 and 33705 (South St. Pete)

South St. Petersburg covers the 33712 and 33705 ZIPs and represents the most affordable single-family entry points in the city. The 33712 ZIP shows a median sale price of approximately $459,000 with 36 days on market and approximately 5.0 months of supply. The 33705 ZIP, which covers a broad swath of south St. Pete, runs meaningfully below that figure, with neighborhoods showing price points accessible to buyers in the $275,000 to $380,000 range.

South St. Pete has been a focus of neighborhood investment activity and gentrification pressure over the past several years. Buyers who bought in south St. Pete in 2018 to 2021 have captured meaningful appreciation. The Deuces corridor along 22nd Street South is one area that has seen commercial and residential reinvestment. Buyers considering this area should verify flood zone designations on a property-by-property basis, as portions of south St. Pete near the bay carry AE and VE flood zone designations with corresponding insurance requirements.

## St. Petersburg Condo Market: What's Different in 2026

The condo segment in St. Petersburg deserves separate treatment because its dynamics in 2026 are materially different from single-family homes. Condo inventory citywide is running at approximately 6.7 months of supply with an average days on market of 52, putting this firmly in buyer's market territory. The driver is the Florida Senate Bill 4-D structural reserve funding mandate.

For buildings that are three stories or taller and more than 25 years old, owners are required to fund structural reserve accounts based on a completed inspection and engineering study. This has pushed sellers in older high-rise and mid-rise buildings to list rather than absorb the coming assessment costs. Buyers of older St. Pete condo units must budget for a milestone inspection, current reserve funding status, and any upcoming special assessments. These are not optional due diligence items; they are essential.

New or recently renovated low-rise condos and townhomes in south St. Pete are less affected because many meet current code. The south-end new-construction condo and townhome segment is actually moving faster than the resale high-rise market.

## Insurance Costs: What Buyers Must Budget

One of the most significant carrying cost differences between St. Petersburg and inland east Hillsborough markets like Valrico is homeowners insurance. St. Pete's proximity to Tampa Bay and the Gulf Coast puts it in a higher-risk tier than most of Hillsborough County.

For a non-waterfront single-family home in St. Pete, a standard HO-3 policy runs approximately $3,500 to $5,500 per year in 2026. Waterfront or bay-adjacent properties add significant flood insurance costs on top of that figure.

By contrast, most of Valrico sits in FEMA Flood Zone X, which carries minimal flood risk and no mandatory flood insurance requirement. A Valrico homeowner in an established subdivision typically pays $2,800 to $4,200 per year for homeowners insurance. The annual gap of $700 to $1,300 between St. Pete and Valrico insurance costs compounds materially over a 10-year hold. Always get insurance quotes before finalizing an offer on any St. Pete property.

For flood zone verification, use FEMA's Flood Map Service Center at [msc.fema.gov](https://msc.fema.gov) on any individual address before writing an offer.

## School Zones vs School Choice: How St. Pete Differs from Valrico

One of the most important structural differences between St. Petersburg and Valrico for family buyers is how school assignment works. Hillsborough County uses a zone-based assignment system. In Valrico, your address determines which school your child attends, and the Newsome High School zone in 33596 carries a measurable home value premium that has been documented consistently over the past decade. See our [Valrico market update for October 2026](/blog/valrico-fl-real-estate-market-update-october-2026) for current pricing data on the Newsome zone premium.

Pinellas County uses a school choice system. You submit an application, rank your preferred schools, and the district assigns students based on availability and sibling priority. There is no automatic assignment based on address for most public schools. This means there is no direct "school zone premium" built into St. Pete property values the way Valrico's Newsome zone commands its consistent pricing premium.

Families who want certainty about school placement and want that school-zone value to be built into their home equity find the Hillsborough zone system more predictable. Families who want flexibility and are comfortable navigating an application process may find the Pinellas choice system workable. Neither system is inherently better; they are fundamentally different mechanisms that affect resale value and buyer behavior differently.

## How Does St. Petersburg Compare to Valrico for Buyers?

St. Pete and Valrico serve different buyer priorities, and there is no universal right answer. Here is the direct comparison.

**Price:** Valrico 33596 carries a median of approximately $468,000. St. Pete's mid-tier ZIPs like 33703 ($415,000 median) and 33712 ($459,000 median) are comparable. Buyers who want to be in St. Pete at the $400,000 price point have real options in the same tier as established Valrico subdivisions.

**Insurance:** Valrico wins on carrying cost. The inland location keeps both homeowners and flood insurance meaningfully lower than most of St. Pete.

**Commute:** St. Pete and Valrico are roughly comparable for Tampa commutes. St. Pete via I-275 runs 25 to 40 minutes to downtown Tampa depending on time of day. Valrico via I-4 and I-275 runs 30 to 50 minutes. Neither has a material advantage for Tampa-based workers.

**Lifestyle:** St. Pete offers walkable neighborhoods, arts venues, beach proximity, and a cultural energy that suburban east Hillsborough does not replicate. Valrico offers newer housing stock in many neighborhoods, larger lots, lower carrying costs, and established school zone premiums that are measurable in resale value.

**School certainty:** Valrico gives you a defined school assignment. St. Pete gives you a choice application process. For families that want a guaranteed school without navigating annual enrollment, Valrico's zone system is more predictable.
